## 1. Financing options (SBA 7(a), 504, state programs, conventional, seller, equipment)

### Takeaway
SBA 7(a) (up to $5M, ~10% max variable rate on loans >$350K at 7.00% prime in Nov 2026) and SBA 504 (CDC debenture ~6.6% fixed in Nov 2026) are the standard tools, but two things dominate this deal: (a) since March 1, 2026 SBA requires 100% ownership by U.S. citizens/nationals — green-card holders cannot own any share — and (b) used-auto-parts and recyclable-material wholesalers are on SBA's environmentally sensitive list, so a Phase I ESA is the starting point. SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective Oct 1, 2026) further tightened acquisitions (1.25x DSCR on historical cash flow, full underwriting for all acquisitions).

### Cited Findings

**SBA 7(a) basics and current rates**
- "The maximum loan amount for a 7(a) loan is $5 million." Eligible uses include real estate, working capital, refinancing, machinery/equipment, furniture/fixtures and **changes of ownership**. Basic eligibility: operating for-profit, US-located, small by SBA size standards, unable to obtain credit elsewhere on reasonable terms, creditworthy. [fetched] — [SBA 7(a) loans](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/7a-loans)
- Maximum variable-rate spreads over base rate shown on SBA's page (listed there for the Working Capital Pilot; secondary sources show the same tiers for standard 7(a)): ≤$50K base+6.5%; $50,001–$250K base+6.0%; $250,001–$350K base+4.5%; >$350K base+3.0%. [fetched] — [SBA 7(a) loans](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/7a-loans)
- Prime rate reported at 7.00% as of early Nov 2026; resulting max variable 7(a) rates: 13.5% (≤$50K), 13.0% ($50,001–$250K), 11.5% ($250,001–$350K), 10.0% (>$350K). [snippet] — [NerdWallet SBA loan rates, Nov 2026](https://www.nerdwallet.com/blog/small-business/sba-loan-rates/); [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/loans/small-business/sba-loan-rates/)
- 7(a) Working Capital Pilot: monitored lines of credit up to $5M, for businesses with ≥1 year operating history; max maturity 60 months; guaranty 85% (≤$150K) / 75% (>$150K). [fetched] — [SBA 7(a) loans](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/7a-loans)
- Full underwriting required for loans over $350,000; hazard insurance typically mandatory above $50,000; life insurance required if the loan is not fully secured by hard collateral. [fetched] — [Live Oak Bank, SOP 50 10 8 guide (updated Oct 28, 2026)](https://www.liveoak.bank/blog/navigating-the-sbas-new-sop-50-10-8)

**SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 1, 2025) — equity injection, seller notes**
- SOP 50 10 8 effective June 1, 2025. Minimum equity injection is 10% for startups AND for complete changes of ownership. [fetched] — [Live Oak Bank](https://www.liveoak.bank/blog/navigating-the-sbas-new-sop-50-10-8)
- A seller note can cover up to 5% of the 10% (i.e., half of the required injection) only if on **full standby (no payments) for the life of the SBA loan**, documented on SBA Form 155. [fetched] — [Live Oak Bank](https://www.liveoak.bank/blog/navigating-the-sbas-new-sop-50-10-8). Note: the pre-2025 rule (2023 procedural notice) only required 24 months of standby; some web sources still repeat "2 years" — that is outdated. [snippet] — [SBA Procedural Notice 5000-846607 (2023)](https://www.sba.gov/sites/sbagov/files/2023-05/2023.05.09%20Procedural%20Notice%205000-846607%20Implementation%20of%20the%20Final%20Rule%20on%20Affiliation%20and%20Lending%20Criteria.pdf); [Windsor Advantage on SOP 50 10 8 equity injection](https://www.windsoradvantage.com/news/updated-sba-equity-injection-rules-what-you-need-to-know-about-sop-50-10-8)
- Partial change of ownership: a seller who keeps ANY equity (even 1%) must give a full personal guarantee for at least two years after the sale; to avoid it the seller must exit to 0%. [fetched] — [Live Oak Bank](https://www.liveoak.bank/blog/navigating-the-sbas-new-sop-50-10-8)
- Acceptable equity documentation: 30–90 days of bank statements, wire confirmations, gift letters with donor statements, ROBS (401(k) rollover) records. [fetched] — [Live Oak Bank](https://www.liveoak.bank/blog/navigating-the-sbas-new-sop-50-10-8)

**SOP 50 10 8.1 (issued Aug 14, 2026; effective Oct 1, 2026) — the currently governing version**
- Applies to all applications issued an SBA loan number on or after October 1, 2026. [fetched] — [PilieroMazza, "Five SBA 7(a) changes…" (Aug 24, 2026)](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions); [Coleman Report](https://colemanreport.com/this-just-in-sba-releases-sop-50-10-8-1-effective-october-1-2026/) [snippet]
- DSCR for most change-of-ownership transactions rises from 1.15x to 1.25x, and projections generally cannot cure a historical cash-flow shortfall. [fetched] — [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions)
- "All acquisitions will be treated as standard 7(a) transactions regardless of their size" — full credit memo, independent valuation, site visit, historical cash-flow test even for loans ≤$350K. [fetched] — [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions)
- Purchase price ≥$3M: Quality of Earnings report mandatory in addition to valuation. [fetched] — [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions); [Doeren Mayhew (Aug 24, 2026)](https://www.doeren.com/viewpoint/sop-50-10-8-1-what-sba-lenders-should-be-thinking-about-before-oct-1)
- Business expansions and partner buyouts now also need "a minimum equity injection of at least 10%". Non-cash equity (qualifying seller notes, third-party notes, standby debt) "shall not collectively represent more than half of the minimum equity injection." Lenders also assess post-closing buyer liquidity. [fetched] — [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions)
- CONFLICT: early summaries of 8.1 claimed first-time buyers could no longer count a 5% seller standby note at all; other analyses say the standby seller note still counts but shares the 50% cap with all other non-cash sources. Unresolved from secondary sources — check SOP text/lender. [snippet] — [Searchfunder thread on SOP 50 10 8.1](https://searchfunder.com/post/new-sop-changes-sop-50-10-81); [MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/sba-stopped-lending-on-projections-sop-50-10-8-1-closes-acquisition-loans-on-last-year-s-numbers)

**SBA citizenship / ownership rule (critical for immigrant owners) — status as of research date**
- SBA's Feb 2, 2026 policy notice ("Update to SOP 50 10 8 – Citizenship and Residency Requirements and Rescission of Procedural Notice 5000-872050"), effective **March 1, 2026**, requires "100% ownership by U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals only" (direct and indirect owners), each with principal residence in the US, its territories or possessions. Lawful permanent residents (green card holders) may not own "any share" — even 1% disqualifies. Also ineligible: undocumented persons, asylees, refugees, visa holders, DACA recipients. Covers 7(a) and 504. [fetched] — [Dickinson Wright client alert (Feb 19, 2026)](https://www.dwlaw.com/news-alerts/client-alert-sba-loan-restrictions-for-noncitizens)
- It rescinded the December 2025 notice (5000-872050) that had allowed up to 5% ownership by foreign nationals/LPRs. [fetched] — [Dickinson Wright](https://www.dwlaw.com/news-alerts/client-alert-sba-loan-restrictions-for-noncitizens); notice PDFs: [NAGGL copy 5000-876441](https://www.naggl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/5000-876441.pdf), [NAGGL copy 5000-876626](https://www.naggl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/5000-876626.pdf) [snippet]
- Requirement extended to SBA Microloan and Surety Bond Guarantee programs from April 1, 2026; borrowers who already hold SBA loans are not affected. [snippet] — [NerdWallet, "Green Card Holders No Longer Eligible for SBA Loans"](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/loans/news/sba-loan-green-card-holders)
- SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective Oct 1, 2026) incorporates this: "SBA financing will be limited to applicants who are U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals"; owners that are entities must be organized in the US. [fetched] — [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions)
- The policy does not bar noncitizens from owning/operating a US business — they must finance outside SBA programs. Law firm estimate: 10–15% of current SBA recipients could be ineligible for new loans/refinancing. [fetched] — [Dickinson Wright](https://www.dwlaw.com/news-alerts/client-alert-sba-loan-restrictions-for-noncitizens)
- Practical effect reported June 2026: lenders take longer because they must verify every owner's citizenship. [snippet] — [NPR, June 12, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/12/nx-s1-5853375/changes-loans-trump-small-business-immigrants-entrepreneurs-green-card)
- I searched for lawsuits/reversals/legislation Sept–Nov 2026 and found none reported; latest coverage found was June 2026 plus the Aug–Oct 2026 SOP 8.1 commentary confirming the rule persists. — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/loans/news/sba-loan-green-card-holders); [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions)

**Acquisition benchmarks relevant to loan sizing**
- BizBuySell sold-business data 2021–2025, junk & salvage yards: median revenue ≈$1.14M; median seller's discretionary earnings ≈$251K; median revenue multiple 1.03x; median earnings multiple 3.85x. [snippet — page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch] — [BizBuySell valuation benchmarks, junk/salvage yard](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/junk-salvage-yard/)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE — median acquisition: $251K SDE × 3.85 ≈ $966K (or 1.03 × $1.14M ≈ $1.17M) for the business, usually excluding real estate. Under SOP 50 10 8/8.1 that implies ≥$97K–$117K equity injection, of which at most half can be non-cash (e.g., a seller note on full life-of-loan standby).
- ESTIMATE — debt service test: $1.0M at 10.0% over 10 years ≈ $13,200/month ≈ $159K/yr. Median SDE of $251K gives 1.58x before paying a manager; if the buyer (already running a dealership) must hire a $80K manager, coverage falls to ~1.08x — below the 1.25x SOP 8.1 threshold. A median yard only clears SBA DSCR if owner-operated, bought below median multiple, or if real estate is included and amortized over 25 years.
- Citizenship rule: a naturalized US citizen owner is eligible; an LPR owner (or any LPR co-owner/spouse with even 1%) makes the applicant ineligible for 7(a), 504, and microloans. The existing dealership and rental company would be affiliates whose ownership also has to be disclosed. If any owner is not a citizen, the realistic stack is conventional bank/credit union debt + seller financing + equipment finance + MGCC/CDFI. The owners' status is unknown to me — the report writer should present both branches.
- Environmental: because the dealer's current NAICS (441 with service bays) is itself on the list, he has probably dealt with environmental questionnaires before; a salvage yard is a step up — expect Phase I always, Phase II likely on any existing yard, and many conventional banks declining to take a salvage-yard parcel as collateral (lender-liability/foreclosure concerns). Leasing the land shifts but does not remove this (lease indemnities, baseline study).
- 504 fits a land purchase + building + crusher/loader (≥10-year life) but not vehicle inventory or working capital; pair with a 7(a) or bank line for those.

## 2. Startup cost breakdown by model

### Takeaway
Published "all-in" estimates for a leased/lean yard cluster around $150K–$750K excluding land purchase, and $0.5M–$2M with land; all are generic-blog or template-vendor numbers, none is MA/RI-specific, and Greater Boston industrial land would push the upper end far higher.

### Cited Findings
- ZenBusiness itemized table (page last updated Sept 9, 2026) [fetched] — [ZenBusiness auto salvage yard guide](https://www.zenbusiness.com/start-a-business/automotive/auto-salvage-yard/):
  - Land purchase or lease deposit: $20,000–$300,000 (text: $100K–$300K for a parcel in a heavy industrial zone, region-dependent)
  - Zoning variance and environmental permitting: $5,000–$20,000
  - Concrete pads and fluid containment: $10,000–$40,000
  - Fluid evacuation station: $8,000–$25,000
  - Wheel loader or forklift: $40,000–$120,000
  - Flatbed tow truck or car hauler: $40,000–$90,000
  - Initial salvage vehicle inventory: $30,000–$100,000
  - Inventory management software: $2,000–$10,000
  - Perimeter fencing and security: $15,000–$40,000
  - Stated total: "about $150,000 for a lean operation to $500,000 or more"; the line items actually sum to ~$170K–$745K (internal inconsistency in the source).
  - Average annual revenue $300K–$2M; break-even 24–48 months; environmental permits "often take three to six months".
- Template vendor (planning assumptions, not market data): $398,000–$1,180,000 for a lean leased yard excluding land; headline "$652k cash plan"; assumes ~$25,000 fluid drainage system, $45,000 heavy-duty forklift, $20,000 crusher lease deposit, and advises not buying a crusher before volume justifies it; "13-month launch roadmap". [snippet] — [Financial Models Lab startup costs](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/startup-costs/junkyard); [13-month roadmap](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/how-to-open/junkyard)
- Another generic guide: $500,000–$2,000,000 total. [snippet] — [junkyardsnearme.net](https://junkyardsnearme.net/auto-salvage-yard-business/)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE (my modelling framing, built from the ranges above; not sourced as totals):
  - *Tow-and-scrap "cash for junk cars" operation* (no retail yard; cars go straight to a shredder/yard): essentially one rollback ($40K–$90K per ZenBusiness, less used), licensing, insurance, phone/web marketing and float to buy cars — on the order of $75K–$200K. The dealer already has dealer license, auction access and probably a truck, lowering this further.
  - *Small full-service dismantler (leased 1–3 acres or indoor)*: ZenBusiness line items minus land purchase ≈ $170K–$450K plus 3–6 months working capital.
  - *Self-service U-Pull (10+ acres)*: needs far more land, stands, inventory depth (hundreds of cars set at once) and a crusher/loader pair; generic sources' $0.5M–$2M is plausibly a floor before land in MA/RI.
- In eastern MA the binding constraint is the site (zoning + Class 3 license + environmental), not the equipment; buying an existing licensed yard converts most "startup" cost into goodwill/real-estate price.

### Gaps
- No MA/RI-specific numbers found for: industrial land price/lease per acre, paving/concrete pad cost per sq ft, oil-water separator installation, stormwater (SWPPP/MSGP Sector M) consultant fees, fencing per linear foot, Class 3 license fees. (Licensing/regulatory items may be covered by another researcher.)
- No sourced breakdown specific to a U-Pull yard or to a tow-and-scrap operation; the three-model split above is my own estimate.
- Yard-management software pricing (Hollander Powerlink, Checkmate/Car-Part, URG, etc.) not retrieved beyond the $2K–$10K generic range.

## 4. Unit economics per vehicle, staffing, revenue scale

### Takeaway
No authoritative per-car P&L was found. What is sourced: hulk scrap ~$120–$200/ton nationally in 2026 (ferrous at a three-year low in late 2025), converters averaging ~$100 with wide dispersion, parts = 70–80% of a full-service recycler's revenue, and median sold yards doing ~$1.14M revenue / ~$251K SDE (~22%).

## 6. Synergies for a used-car dealer and lower-capital entry paths

### Takeaway
No source quantified dealer–salvage synergies; the sourced facts point toward a staged entry — the industry itself advises against buying a crusher or heavy site until volume justifies it, and a tow/buy-and-resell-to-shredder or in-shop parts model avoids the land, licensing-site, and SBA-environmental hurdles.

### Cited Findings
- Template vendor guidance: budget a crusher lease/outsourced access rather than purchase — "don't force a purchase before volume justifies it". [snippet] — [Financial Models Lab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/startup-costs/junkyard)
- Key operating risks named for new yards: overbidding at auction through misjudged parts demand; title/sales recordkeeping lapses that can jeopardize the dismantler license in year one; cash-flow gap before parts revenue builds; seasonality (insurance-auction inventory rises in winter in cold states; parts demand peaks spring/summer). [fetched] — [ZenBusiness](https://www.zenbusiness.com/start-a-business/automotive/auto-salvage-yard/)
- High-margin parts are drivetrain components from late-model vehicles (60–80%); low-value panels on older imports barely cover labor. [fetched] — [ZenBusiness](https://www.zenbusiness.com/start-a-business/automotive/auto-salvage-yard/)
- Dealer-with-service-bays (NAICS 441) and auto repair (8111) sites are already SBA "environmentally sensitive", i.e., doing dismantling in the existing shop does not put the property in a new SBA category the way a new yard parcel would. [fetched PDF] — [Partner ESI / SOP 50 10 8 Appendix 6](https://www.partneresi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Partner-ESI-SBA-SOP-50-10-8-with-TU-NAICS-Codes.pdf)

### Inferences
- Synergies (qualitative, my reasoning): (1) the dealer and rental fleet are captive consumers of used parts for reconditioning; (2) auction mistakes/unsellable trade-ins get a higher exit value (part-out + scrap) than wholesale-to-junk; (3) existing auction access, title clerks, tow relationships and repair bays reduce incremental overhead; (4) the rental fleet's total-loss/high-mileage units become feedstock.
- Lower-capital sequence worth modelling: (a) junk-car buying/brokerage reselling whole to an existing licensed yard or shredder → (b) selective part-out of a few high-value vehicles inside the existing shop with online parts sales (eBay/Car-Part) → (c) lease or acquire a licensed yard once volume is proven. Each step must be checked against MA licence class rules (Class 2 vs Class 3) by the regulatory researcher — I did not verify what a Class 2 dealer may dismantle.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on eBay Motors used-parts seller economics (fees, average order value), on Copart/IAA buyer fees and licence requirements for MA dealers, or on rebuilder (salvage-to-rebuilt title) margins in MA.
- No examples/case studies found of used-car dealers partnering with existing yards.

## 7. Main risks, failure causes, and timeline

### Takeaway
Sourced risks are commodity-price swings, auction overbidding, licensing/recordkeeping, environmental liability, zoning/permitting and the pre-revenue cash gap; sourced timelines are 3–6 months for environmental permits, ~13 months decision-to-open in one planning template, and 24–48 months to break even.

### Cited Findings
- Risks: scrap price volatility outside operator control; overbidding at auction; recordkeeping/compliance lapses threatening the dismantler licence; contamination claims, fines and heavy-machinery injuries; permitting/zoning complications even on zoned sites (deed restrictions, proximity rules); cash-flow gap; seasonality. [fetched] — [ZenBusiness](https://www.zenbusiness.com/start-a-business/automotive/auto-salvage-yard/)
- Ferrous scrap sat at a three-year low in Q4 2025. [fetched] — [ARA Market & Metals Q4 2025](https://www.a-r-a.org/news/market-metals-quarter-4-2025/)
- Environmental permits often take 3–6 months before the first vehicle can be processed; break-even 24–48 months. [fetched] — [ZenBusiness](https://www.zenbusiness.com/start-a-business/automotive/auto-salvage-yard/)
- A planning template frames opening a junkyard as a 13-month roadmap. [snippet] — [Financial Models Lab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/how-to-open/junkyard)
- Financing-side risks: SBA DSCR raised to 1.25x on historical results for acquisitions; all acquisitions fully underwritten; noncompliance with SBA environmental policy can cost the lender its guaranty (so lenders are strict); citizenship rule can disqualify the applicant outright. [fetched/snippet] — [PilieroMazza](https://pilieromazza.com/five-sba-7a-changes-that-could-reshape-business-acquisitions); [Dickinson Wright](https://www.dwlaw.com/news-alerts/client-alert-sba-loan-restrictions-for-noncitizens)

- ESTIMATE — timeline: acquisition of an existing licensed yard with SBA financing ≈ 4–9 months (LOI → Phase I/II → valuation → SBA closing → licence transfer); greenfield yard in eastern MA ≈ 12–24+ months driven by zoning/special permit and local licence hearings, consistent with the 13-month template plus New England permitting friction. Not sourced.
