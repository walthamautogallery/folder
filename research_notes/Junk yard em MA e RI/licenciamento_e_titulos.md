## 1. Massachusetts — Class 3 license (MGL c.140 §§57-69), how it differs from Class 1/2, junk dealer license, catalytic converter law

### Takeaway
In Massachusetts the dismantler/junk yard license is the municipal "Class 3 – motor vehicle junk license" under MGL c.140 §§57-59, issued by the city/town licensing authority (not the state), expiring every January 1, capped at $200/year, and requiring a public hearing with 7 days' notice to abutters for a first license; siting is constrained by §59A (inside a building, or distance/screening tests) and by local zoning. A Class 2 license does not cover dismantling-for-parts as a principal business; unlicensed operation is a crime (fine $200-$1,000 and/or up to 1 year).

### Cited Findings

**Who needs a license (§57)**
- A license is required to "engage in the business of buying, selling, exchanging or assembling second hand motor vehicles or parts thereof"; the section also covers auctions and lease/rental companies selling to the public. Only manufacturers of new vehicles and finance/insurance companies with incidental used sales are exempt. — [MGL c.140 §57](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section57)
- §57 also says sales of used vehicles or parts by covered persons must be "reported weekly to the registrar of motor vehicles" on prescribed forms. — [MGL c.140 §57](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section57)

**The three classes (§58)**
- Class 1: recognized agent of a manufacturer under written contract, principal business new vehicles, used sales incidental. — [MGL c.140 §58](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section58)
- Class 2 (used car dealer): a person "whose principal business is the buying or selling of second hand motor vehicles"; must maintain a $25,000 surety bond (or equivalent: CD or irrevocable letter of credit accepted by the licensing authority); a separate bond for each business name and each city/town; no license issued or renewed without a valid bond; 10 days to cure if the surety cancels. — [MGL c.140 §58](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section58)
- Class 3 (motor vehicle junk license): a person "whose principal business is the buying of second hand motor vehicles" for remodeling, taking apart or rebuilding and selling them, or the buying or selling of parts of second-hand vehicles or tires, or assembling second-hand vehicle parts. No bond is stated in §58 for Class 3. — [MGL c.140 §58](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section58)

**Issuance, term, fee, hearing, revocation, appeal (§59)**
- Issuer: the police commissioner in Boston and "the licensing authorities in other cities and towns". — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Term: licenses "shall expire on January first following the date of issue unless sooner revoked" (i.e., annual, calendar-year). — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Fee: set by the local board/officer; "in no event shall any such fee be greater than $200". — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Hearing: "No original license or permit shall be issued hereunder to a person within said class 3 until after a hearing", with seven days' notice to owners of abutting property. Outside Boston the board may waive the annual hearing on renewal of a Class 3 license. — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Premises: the license must specify all premises; changes/additions need a written permit attached to the license. "Cities and towns by ordinance or by-law may regulate the situation of the premises of licensees within class 3." — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Revocation: mandatory after hearing if the licensee is not complying with law/regulations; no new license to that person or for the same premises without the Registrar's approval; revocations reported to the Registrar. Appeal of refusal/revocation: within 10 days to Superior Court. — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Boston practice: public notice and hearing are required for new Class 3 (junk) dealers (snippet). — [Boston Police – become an approved motor vehicle dealer](https://police.boston.gov/become-an-approved-motor-vehicle-dealer/)
- Examples of local fees (not Waltham): Braintree $75 filing + $100 license; Littleton $100; Maynard $200 (snippets). — [Braintree](https://braintreema.gov/DocumentCenter/View/13021); [Littleton](https://littletonma.org/503/Motorized-Vehicle-License); [Maynard](https://townofmaynard-ma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/586/Application-to-buy-sell-exchange-or-assemble-second-hand-motor-vehicles-PDF)

**Siting conditions specific to Class 3 (§59A)**
- A Class 3 junkyard license may be granted only if the yard meets one of these alternatives: (A) operated entirely within a building; (B) operated only to salvage scrap value (not to resell parts for their original purpose) and located in a built-up industrial/commercial area, or contiguous to a railroad siding or docking facilities; (C) more than 1,000 ft from the nearest edge of any interstate/primary highway, more than 600 ft from any other state highway, and more than 300 ft from any park, bathing beach, playground, school, church or cemetery and not within ordinary view of them; or (D) screened by natural objects or well-constructed, maintained fences at least 6 ft high, acceptable to the city/town and compliant with state highway department regulations, and specified on the license. For alternative D a copy of the application goes to the state highway department within 3 working days of filing. — [MGL c.140 §59A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59A)

**Record-keeping ("police book") and inspection (§§62, 66)**
- Every licensee must keep, on the licensed premises, a book in a form approved by the Registrar, with entries "legibly written in the English language" for every purchase, sale, exchange or receipt for sale: description of the vehicle or parts, the seller, purchaser, alleged owner, identifying numbers, and whether any numbers were removed, defaced, altered or destroyed. An electronic data management system in a Registrar-approved form is allowed instead; the Registrar may demand printouts for a specific vehicle, for vehicles on the premises at inspection, or for the previous seven days. — [MGL c.140 §62](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section62)
- State police, the Attorney General (or designee), the Boston police commissioner, local police chiefs, selectmen and officers they authorize may enter licensed premises "at any time" to examine vehicles, parts, books, papers and inventories. — [MGL c.140 §66](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section66)

**Penalties (§§68, 69)**
- §68: carrying on a §57 business without a license, operating at a place or in a manner other than as licensed, or continuing after revocation/suspension: fine of $200 to $1,000, imprisonment up to one year, or both; after two or more convictions the Attorney General is notified for action under c.93A §6. — [MGL c.140 §68](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section68)
- §69: any other violation of §§57-68 or of Registrar rules under §60: fine up to $500, imprisonment up to one year, or both; convictions reported to the Registrar, who may suspend/revoke registration certificates (dealer plates) issued under c.90 §5. — [MGL c.140 §69](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section69)
- §64 was repealed (St. 1996, c.429). — [MGL c.140 §64](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section64)

**Junk dealer / second-hand metals license (§54) and Waltham**
- Cities and towns may by ordinance/by-law license "collectors of, dealers in or keepers of shops for the purchase, sale or barter of junk, old metals or second hand articles"; the authority may attach rules and restrictions stated in the license; licenses "may be revoked at pleasure". — [MGL c.140 §54](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section54)
- Waltham's code (Chapter 8, Article IV, junk collectors and junk dealers) cites MGL c.140 §§54-56; a Waltham section requires a license "from the City Council" for handling junk with a $100 application fee paid to the City Clerk (snippet; page itself returned 403). — [Waltham code, ecode360](https://ecode360.com/26931848)
- Waltham treats a junk motor vehicle stored on private property more than 30 days as a nuisance, with an exemption for wrecked/inoperative vehicles on the site of a licensed business in an appropriate zoning district (snippet). — [Waltham code, ecode360](https://ecode360.com/26931838)

**Catalytic converters (MGL c.266 §148, added by St. 2022 c.407)**
- Applies to persons licensed under c.140 §§54, 54A, 56 or 58 (so Class 1/2/3 licensees and junk dealers) or c.140B §1. They may not acquire a catalytic converter unless the seller presents photo ID plus a bill of sale or other document showing ownership, and may not buy from anyone who is not a commercial enterprise or the owner of the vehicle the converter came from. — [MGL c.266 §148](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter266/Section148)
- Records per transaction: date; seller's name, address, phone, signature; copy of photo ID; license plate of the delivery vehicle; description and photograph of the converter; copies of ownership documents; price per converter and total. Retain 3 years; produce to the local police chief on request. — [MGL c.266 §148](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter266/Section148)
- Payment must be by company check for transactions over $250 per customer per day or involving 3 or more converters. The ID/record rules do not apply to purchases of whole motor vehicles. — [MGL c.266 §148](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter266/Section148)
- Enforcement by local police via civil fines: $100 per converter in violation plus $300 (first offense), $550 (second), $700 (subsequent); the municipal licensing board may suspend or revoke the license; 36 violation-free months resets to first offense. — [MGL c.266 §148](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter266/Section148)
- The act was signed January 5, 2023 (snippet). — [mass.gov, MGL c.266 §148 page](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/mass-general-laws-c266-ss-148)
- Related: c.266 §142 covers records of purchases by dealers in scrap copper wire; §142A covers gold/silver/platinum dealers, not converters (snippet). — [ISRI/ReMA Massachusetts resources](https://www.isri.org/advocacy-compliance/state-policies/state-resources-tracking/state-specific-policy-resources-massachusetts)

### Inferences
- Class 2 + Class 3 together: §59 refers to the applicant being a proper person for "the classifications for which he has applied" (plural), and nothing read in §§57-59 prohibits holding both. Combined with the "principal business" wording of §58, the practical reading is that a Class 2 dealer who starts buying vehicles to dismantle and sell parts needs a separate Class 3 license for the premises where that occurs. This is an inference; the statute text read does not expressly say dual licensing is allowed, and local boards decide.
- A Class 3 at the existing Waltham dealership lot is not automatic: it needs a new hearing with abutter notice, a §59A-qualifying site (most realistically "entirely within a building" or 6-ft screening in an urban setting), and zoning that allows the use.
- Because the license is tied to listed premises and expires each January 1, a second yard in another town means a second municipal license.
- Selling scrap metal and loose converters may additionally trigger the local junk dealer license (§54) depending on the municipality's ordinance.

### Gaps
- Waltham's exact Class 3 fee, application form, licensing body for §59 motor vehicle licenses (License Commission vs City Council) and zoning districts permitting auto salvage could not be verified; the ecode360 pages returned 403 and no Waltham Class 3 form surfaced. Ask the Waltham City Clerk / License Commission and the Building/Zoning Department.
- Whether Waltham has any existing Class 3 licensees or a local cap was not found.
- 540 CMR dealer-related rules beyond salvage inspection, and RMV rules made under c.140 §60, were not read.
- MassDEP / EPA environmental obligations (stormwater, fluids, mercury switches, refrigerant) are outside this scope and not researched here.

## 2. Massachusetts — RMV salvage titles, "parts only", salvage inspection, owner-retained total loss

### Takeaway
Massachusetts brands total-loss vehicles as salvage "Repairable" or "Parts Only"; a Parts-Only vehicle can never be re-titled or registered in MA and is fit only for dismantling, while a Repairable one must pass a State Police salvage inspection (540 CMR 12.00, $50 fee) before it can be titled as reconstructed. The insurer chooses the brand.

### Cited Findings
- "Parts only" = a vehicle declared a total loss and unrepairable; it "can never be re-titled or registered in Massachusetts"; the insurer that declared the total loss determines whether the vehicle is branded Parts-Only (snippet). — [mass.gov – Title types and definitions](https://www.mass.gov/service-details/title-types-and-definitions)
- RMV training material states a parts-only vehicle must only be used or sold for parts (snippet). — [RMV EVR salvage reference guide](https://www.mass.gov/doc/evr-salvage-reference-guide/download)
- The salvage inspection procedure is set out in 540 CMR 12.00 (Inspection of restored salvage motor vehicles) (snippet). — [mass.gov – Salvage inspections](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/salvage-inspections); [540 CMR 12.00](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/540-CMR-1200-inspection-of-restored-salvage-motor-vehicles)
- Salvage inspection fee is $50, paid at an RMV Service Center, after which the applicant contacts the Massachusetts State Police Salvage Unit; inspections are by appointment (snippet). — [RMV salvage handout](https://www.mass.gov/doc/rmv-salvage-handout/download)
- Owner-retained title: for a total-loss vehicle the owner keeps; the vehicle must have an active registration and be legally and safely operable at the time of loss; application due within 10 days of receiving the insurance settlement; no salvage inspection required; not needed for passenger vehicles at least 10 model years old at the time of damage (snippet). — [mass.gov – Apply for an owner-retained title](https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-an-owner-retained-title)
- If the owner keeps a total-loss vehicle that is not operable, it must get a Salvage Title with either a Parts Only or Repairable brand (snippet). — [mass.gov – Total loss and salvage vehicles](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/total-loss-and-salvage-vehicles)

### Inferences
- For a recycler, Parts-Only units are the natural feedstock (cheaper at auction, no rebuild path in MA); Repairable units leave the option to rebuild and retail through the existing Class 2 license after inspection.
- Parts receipts carrying donor-vehicle VINs matter downstream: rebuilders need them at salvage inspection, so a parts seller's invoices should always show the source VIN (RI's DMV states this expressly; see section 3).

### Gaps
- mass.gov pages were blocked, so the following are unverified: the statutory total-loss definition (believed to be in MGL c.90D), salvage title application fee, who may buy/hold salvage titles, exact contents of 540 CMR 12.00, and the surrender/"junk"/destruction reporting procedure when a vehicle is crushed. No 540 CMR section number specific to "parts only" was confirmed (the task's "540 CMR 4.09" guess was not found in search).
- Whether MA requires a specific RMV form to cancel a title when a vehicle is scrapped was not found.

## 3. Rhode Island — licenses, fees, bond, location approval, salvage titles, catalytic converters

### Takeaway
Rhode Island uses a two-layer system: a state Auto Wrecking and Salvage Yard license from the Department of Business Regulation (RIGL 42-14.2; 230-RICR-30-05-5) with a $10,000 surety bond, plus a prerequisite local license under RIGL 5-21 (second-hand/junk dealer) and a zoning letter. If the town has no such local license, the site must meet strict statutory standards (1,000/600/300-ft setbacks, 6-ft fence, minimum 2 contiguous acres). Unlicensed operation is a misdemeanor.

### Cited Findings

**State license**
- "No person shall establish or operate an auto wrecking yard or auto salvage yard without a license therefor as provided in this chapter and in chapter 21 of title 5" (snippet). — [RIGL 42-14.2-3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE42/42-14.2/42-14.2-3.htm)
- DBR: an Auto Salvage License is required for any property with an RI physical address "where the activity of dismantling, shredding, and storing unregistered motor vehicles for sale of parts or metals is actively occurring". — [DBR Auto Wrecking and Salvage FAQ (PDF)](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- Application via eLicensing (elicensing.ri.gov). Owners and managers must submit RI criminal background reports; out-of-state residents must also submit one from their home state. The application also asks about Department of Environmental Management compliance and payment of taxes. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- Fee per DBR FAQ: "$750.00 per location", renewal fee also $750.00; plus a surety bond with $10,000 in coverage. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- Fee per the regulation text: $180.00 payable to the General Treasurer, refundable if denied, first term pro-rated; license valid up to three years; renewal application due at least 30 days before expiry; bond $10,000 from a Director-approved surety, drawable only after other remedies are exhausted. — [230-RICR-30-05-5](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/230-30-05-5)
- A 2014 Justia copy of §42-14.2-6 shows a $250 annual fee (snippet). — [Justia, RIGL 42-14.2-6 (2014)](https://law.justia.com/codes/rhode-island/2014/title-42/chapter-42-14.2/section-42-14.2-6/). CONFLICT: three different fee figures ($180 regulation; $250/yr statute as of 2014; $750 FAQ). A plausible reconciliation is $250/yr x 3-year term = $750, but this was not confirmed; treat $750 per location per (three-year) term as DBR's operative figure and confirm with DBR (401-462-9506).
- Regulation effective date: January 4, 2022 periodic refile with no text change. — [230-RICR-30-05-5](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/230-30-05-5)

**Local license and location approval**
- Applicants must submit (1) a city/town document confirming zoning compliance and (2) a city/town document showing compliance with RIGL 5-21-1 et seq.; if the town does not license under that chapter, the applicant must meet §42-14.2-8(2). — [230-RICR-30-05-5](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/230-30-05-5)
- §42-14.2-8: no new license unless the applicant has obtained a license under local second-hand dealer ordinances enacted under §5-21-1; where the municipality does not issue such licenses, the location must be: more than 1,000 ft from the nearest edge of any interstate/primary highway; more than 600 ft from any other state highway; more than 300 ft from any park, bathing beach, playground, school, church or cemetery and not within view; screened and enclosed by a maintained fence at least 6 ft high (unless a natural barrier screens it); at least 2 acres in one contiguous lot; and described by survey plan, assessor's map or aerial chart. — [RIGL 42-14.2-8](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE42/42-14.2/42-14.2-8.htm)
- DBR: the local license "may be called a second hand dealers license, a junk shop license, or something different"; if the town issues a local license for the property, the property need not meet the physical requirements; if the town issues such licenses but has denied one to the applicant or property, "you cannot apply for a DBR auto salvage license or engage in auto salvage activity". Get the zoning letter before applying. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)

**Operating rules**
- Must operate from a building on the premises with at least 400 sq ft of ground-level floor space, including an office where records are kept at all times. — [230-RICR-30-05-5](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/230-30-05-5)
- Records (kept on premises 2 years, open to DBR inspection in business hours): acquisition, disposition and sale dates; seller's name and residence; purchaser's name and ID method; VIN, make, model, year, color, title type, disposition; parts records. Sellers must be identified by operator's license, Social Security card or other accepted ID. Mobile crushers may not crush on unlicensed premises; crushing records must show date, who crushed, and disposition of material. — [230-RICR-30-05-5](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/230-30-05-5)
- License must be conspicuously displayed; license number must appear on all business communications including vehicles, estimates and signs; licensee must notify DBR immediately of any change, including email address. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- License is not transferable without DBR approval, and a licensed owner cannot lease the property to others to conduct salvage activity. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- Additional grounds for denial/suspension/revocation: failing to answer a DBR information request within 10 days, dishonesty or incompetency, violating a DBR order, certain felony convictions of an owner/manager/employee, failure to supervise employees. — [230-RICR-30-05-5](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/230-30-05-5)

**Penalties**
- §42-14.2-13: any violation of chapter 42-14.2 is a misdemeanor, fine up to $500, imprisonment up to one year, or both, per violation. — [RIGL 42-14.2-13](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE42/42-14.2/42-14.2-13.htm)
- DBR says it works with newly discovered unlicensed operators toward licensing, wind-up or clean-up, including "any appropriate fines or back fees"; it needs actual evidence of storage and dismantling to act, and cannot act solely against someone who advertises to buy junk cars and transports them to a licensed yard. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)

**Salvage titles and rebuilders (RIGL 31-46)**
- Two salvage classes: Class A = extensive damage, good for "parts only"; Class B = considerable damage but repairable. Insurers classify; the DMV administrator may review and reassess. — [RIGL 31-46-1.1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE31/31-46/31-46-1.1.htm)
- Salvage rebuilder license: issued by DBR to auto body repair facilities meeting its equipment/training guidelines; only licensed businesses or individuals may rebuild salvage vehicles; a vehicle repaired or rebuilt by an unlicensed party has its title stamped "FOR PARTS ONLY"; violation is a felony, up to 2 years and/or fine up to $5,000; a numbered license form accompanies each salvage vehicle sold to a licensed rebuilder and must be presented at registration. — [RIGL 31-46-7](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE31/31-46/31-46-7.htm)
- A total-loss vehicle cannot be registered unless a red salvage title certificate is obtained from DMV Title/Research (proof of ownership only), it passes a DMV salvage inspection, and it is issued a title branded "Reconstructed Salvage" or "Rebuilt Salvage"; the vehicle must first pass state safety and emissions inspection; receipts for replaced parts must list the VIN of the vehicle each part came from; inspection fee $58.50 by certified check/money order (snippet). — [RI DMV – Salvage inspections](https://dmv.ri.gov/registrations-plates-titles/inspections-emissions/salvage-inspections)
- Owner-retained total loss: insurer's letter must state total loss, owner retention, and Class A or Class B (snippet). — [RI DMV – Salvage inspections](https://dmv.ri.gov/registrations-plates-titles/inspections-emissions/salvage-inspections)

**Catalytic converters / metals**
- Chapter 6-11.1 (purchase and sale of precious metals) defines precious metals to include catalytic converters when bought from the general public; licensing is by the Attorney General, who shall not license a person without a registered permanent place of business in the state (snippet). — [RIGL 6-11.1-1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-11.1/6-11.1-1.htm)
- 2022 H 7751 (signed June 30, 2022) added converter-specific record-keeping: vehicle registration or VIN plus license plate, state of issue, make and model of the source vehicle (snippet; substitute versions differ in detail). — [2022 H 7751 Sub A](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText22/HouseText22/H7751A.htm); [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/RI/text/H7751/id/2534915)
- §6-11.1-5 requires licensees to hold precious metals, including catalytic converters, unaltered for 14 days (snippet); the AG regulation text mentions 7 days after notifying local police (snippet). CONFLICT between 14-day statute and 7-day regulation; verify. — [Justia, RIGL 6-11.1-5](https://law.justia.com/codes/rhode-island/title-6/chapter-6-11-1/section-6-11-1-5); [110-RICR-20-00-1](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/Regulations/Part/110-20-00-1)
- Chapter 6-11.2 governs purchase and sale of regulated (scrap) metals, §§6-11.2-1 to 6-11.2-17 (snippet). — [ISRI/ReMA Rhode Island resources](https://www.isri.org/advocacy-compliance/advocacy-agenda/state-policies/state-resources-tracking/state-specific-policy-resources-rhode-island)

### Inferences
- A Massachusetts-based operator cannot use a MA Class 3 to dismantle in RI: the RI license attaches to an RI physical address, needs local approval first, and requires home-state plus RI background checks for owners/managers.
- In RI the town effectively holds a veto (local 5-21 license or denial blocks the DBR license), so site selection should start with the municipality, not DBR.
- The 2-acre/setback standards only bite in towns without a local junk/second-hand license; in towns with one, local ordinance governs siting.
- A yard removing converters from vehicles it owns is in a different position from one buying loose converters from the public; the latter appears to pull in the AG precious-metals license. Whether a licensed salvage yard is exempt was not confirmed.

## 4. Federal — NMVTIS, Highway Beautification Act junkyard control, catalytic converter legislation, auction access

### Takeaway
Any junk/salvage yard or recycler handling five or more junk/salvage vehicles a year must report every such vehicle to NMVTIS at least monthly through an approved data consolidator (a free option exists), with federal civil penalties up to $1,000 per unreported vehicle. Federal highway law (23 USC 136) drives the 1,000-ft screening rule that both MA (c.140 §59A, c.140B) and RI (42-14.2-8) implement. There is still no enacted federal catalytic converter statute as of the latest status found (PART Act pending). Copart states that licensed dealers/dismantlers can bid directly in MA and RI; unlicensed buyers need a broker.

### Cited Findings

**NMVTIS (28 CFR 25.56; 49 USC 30504-30505)**
- Who: any individual or entity engaged in the business of operating a junk yard or salvage yard, including auto recyclers; BJA says this includes salvage pools and salvage auctions. — [28 CFR 25.56 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/25.56); [BJA – For auto recyclers, salvage yards and junk yards](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/es/node/36)
- Threshold: those handling fewer than five salvage, junk or total-loss vehicles per year are not required to report. — [28 CFR 25.56](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/25.56)
- Timing: monthly inventory of all junk or salvage automobiles obtained in the prior month; BJA says reporting may be more frequent but not less than monthly, and supplemental disposition reports may be needed at or within 30 days of disposition. — [28 CFR 25.56](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/25.56); [BJA recyclers page](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/es/node/36)
- Data: reporter's name/address/contact; VIN; date obtained; name of person/entity obtained from; disposition (crushed, sold, etc.), recipient, and whether intended for export. — [28 CFR 25.56](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/25.56)
- Exceptions: no duplicate report if the yard already reports the same data to its state and the state passes it to the operator, or if an authorized third party reports it; no report needed when a 49 USC 33110 non-stolen verification is received; vehicles that an independent qualified appraiser determines do not meet the junk/salvage definition. Scrap processors/shredders need not report vehicles already reported by the source. — [28 CFR 25.56](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/25.56)
- Yards must report all salvage or junk vehicles obtained, including vehicles from or on behalf of insurers that can reasonably be assumed to be total losses. — [28 CFR 25.56](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/28/25.56)
- Providers (data consolidators) listed by BJA: AAMVA Single VIN Reporting Service (nmvtisreporting.aamva.org); Solera/Audatex; Auto Data Direct, Inc. (add123.com, described as "Free and Full Service NMVTIS Reporting"); ISO ClaimSearch. Costs other than ADD's free tier are not stated. — [BJA recyclers page](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/es/node/36)
- Penalty: civil penalty of not more than $1,000 for each violation; DOJ applies it per vehicle (e.g., 100 unreported vehicles = up to $100,000); the Attorney General weighs business size and gravity. NMVTIS reporting does not replace state reporting requirements (snippet). — [BJA NMVTIS enforcement policy](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/sites/g/files/xyckuh261/files/media/document/nmvtisenforcementpolicyfinal.pdf); [BJA law enforcement guide](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/sites/g/files/xyckuh261/files/media/document/law-enforcement-and-vehicle-title-investigator-guide.pdf)

**Highway Beautification Act junkyard control (23 USC 136) and state implementation**
- Junkyards within 1,000 ft of the nearest edge of the right-of-way of the Interstate or primary system and visible from the main traveled way must be screened (natural objects, plantings, fences) or removed; exceptions for areas zoned industrial or unzoned industrial areas as determined by the state with federal approval; states failing to provide effective control lose 7 percent of federal-aid highway apportionments; just compensation for removal of lawfully established yards (75% federal share). "Automobile graveyard" = establishment storing, buying or selling wrecked, scrapped or dismantled vehicles or parts. — [23 USC 136 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/23/136)
- Massachusetts implementation: MGL c.140B defines junk, automobile graveyard and junkyard by reference to Title 23, and restricts junkyards with any portion within 1,000 ft of the right-of-way of an interstate/primary highway unless screened, zoned industrial, in an unzoned industrial area, or not visible. — [MGL c.140B §1](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140B/Section1); [MGL c.140B §2](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140B/Section2)
- MA Class 3 licensing layers on stricter state tests (1,000/600/300 ft or 6-ft fencing or indoor operation). — [MGL c.140 §59A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59A)
- Rhode Island mirrors the distances inside its salvage-yard statute. — [RIGL 42-14.2-8](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE42/42-14.2/42-14.2-8.htm)

**Federal catalytic converter legislation**
- No evidence the PART Act (Preventing Auto Recycling Theft Act) has been enacted. H.R. 5221 was introduced September 9, 2025; latest action found is a February 10, 2026 subcommittee voice vote forwarding it to full committee; Senate companion S. 2238. The bill would require VIN/unique ID marking on converters, bar cash or cryptocurrency purchases, and fund a marking grant program (snippet, advocacy/tracker sources). — [NTEA](https://www.ntea.com/articles/association-news/vehicle-parts-theft-legislation-progresses-house-catalytic-converter-bill); [Legisletter H.R. 5221](https://legisletter.org/bill/hr5221-part-act); [NADA](https://www.nada.org/legislative/fight-rising-catalytic-converter-theft)

**Auction access (Copart; IAA not verified)**
- Massachusetts: "Without business licenses, you will need a Broker's help to bid on clean and salvage titled vehicles in Massachusetts." Dealer licenses (Class I and II), the dismantler license (Class III) and the general business license are issued by the local city or town; sales and use tax registration comes from the MA Department of Revenue (snippet). — [Copart – Massachusetts licensing requirements](https://copart.com/fr-CA/content/us/fr-ca/licensing/united-states/massachusetts)
- Rhode Island: "Without business licenses, you will need a Broker's help to bid on clean and salvage titled vehicles in Rhode Island"; dealers, dismantlers, general businesses and exporters need an operational license and a sales tax license at the same physical address; DMV issues dealer licenses, DBR issues dismantler, rebuilder and business licenses; boats, jet skis and snowmobiles can be bought without a broker (snippet from a Copart-affiliated site). — [CrashedToys/Copart – Rhode Island licensing requirements](https://www.crashedtoys.com/es/Content/US/EN/Licensing/United-States/Rhode-Island)
- Copart says bidding eligibility is driven by state law and the licenses on file with the member account (snippet). — [Copart licensing overview](https://www.copart.com/Licensing)

### Inferences
- The existing Class 2 license (plus MA sales tax registration) is already an accepted "business license" for Copart bidding on clean and salvage units in MA; the open question is whether specific lots (e.g., Parts-Only / certificate-of-destruction) are restricted to dismantler (Class 3) accounts.
- Since NMVTIS covers anyone "in the business" of handling 5+ junk/salvage/total-loss vehicles a year for resale whole or as parts, rebuilding or crushing, the dealership may already have a reporting duty on salvage auction purchases even before opening a yard; registering with a consolidator is cheap insurance.
- NMVTIS, the MA police book (§62), the weekly Registrar report (§57) and the §148 converter log are four separate record streams; a yard-management system that produces all four from one intake record is the practical compliance solution.

## 5. Penalties for operating unlicensed and common compliance pitfalls

### Takeaway
Both states criminalise unlicensed dismantling (MA: $200-$1,000 and/or 1 year, with AG referral on repeat; RI: misdemeanor, up to $500 and/or 1 year per violation; RI unlicensed rebuilding is a felony), and federal NMVTIS penalties accrue per vehicle. The most likely traps for an existing Class 2 dealer are operating outside the scope/premises of the current license, siting, and missing one of the parallel record-keeping duties.

### Cited Findings
- MA: unlicensed business, or operating at a place or in a manner other than as licensed, or after revocation: $200-$1,000 fine and/or up to one year; two or more convictions trigger notice to the Attorney General for c.93A §6 action. — [MGL c.140 §68](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section68)
- MA: other violations of §§57-68 or Registrar rules: up to $500 and/or one year, and the Registrar may suspend/revoke c.90 §5 registrations (dealer plates) after a hearing. — [MGL c.140 §69](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section69)
- MA: revocation bars a new license to that person or premises without Registrar approval. — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- MA converters: civil fines of $100 per converter plus $300/$550/$700 by offense, and license suspension/revocation by the municipal board. — [MGL c.266 §148](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter266/Section148)
- RI: misdemeanor, up to $500 and/or one year per violation of chapter 42-14.2. — [RIGL 42-14.2-13](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE42/42-14.2/42-14.2-13.htm)
- RI: rebuilding salvage without a salvage rebuilder license is a felony, up to two years and/or $5,000, and the title is stamped "FOR PARTS ONLY". — [RIGL 31-46-7](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE31/31-46/31-46-7.htm)
- Federal: up to $1,000 per unreported vehicle under NMVTIS (snippet). — [BJA NMVTIS enforcement policy](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/sites/g/files/xyckuh261/files/media/document/nmvtisenforcementpolicyfinal.pdf)
- Pitfalls evidenced in the sources:
  - Scope/premises: §68 punishes a licensee who operates "in any other place or manner" than specified in the license. — [MGL c.140 §68](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section68)
  - Warrantless inspection "at any time" of vehicles, parts, books and inventories. — [MGL c.140 §66](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section66)
  - Missed annual renewal (MA licenses lapse every January 1). — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
  - RI: leasing a licensed yard to another operator, or letting non-employees dismantle on site, is prohibited; mobile crushing on unlicensed premises is prohibited; license number must be on all communications. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf); [230-RICR-30-05-5](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/regulations/Part/230-30-05-5)
  - RI: applying to DBR before securing the zoning letter and local license; a local denial blocks the state license. — [DBR FAQ](https://www.DBR.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
  - Converters: paying cash above $250/day or for 3+ converters in MA; buying loose converters from non-owners. — [MGL c.266 §148](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter266/Section148)

### Inferences
- Stripping parts from auction cars at the existing Waltham lot under only the Class 2 license is the single most exposed scenario: it is arguably operating in a "manner" other than licensed (§68), puts the Class 2 license and dealer plates at risk (§§59, 69), and can also violate the Waltham junk-vehicle nuisance ordinance if units sit dismantled.
- Abutter opposition at the Class 3 hearing and zoning are the real gating items in Greater Boston, not the fee ($200 cap) or bond (none for Class 3 in §58).
- Entering RI is materially costlier upfront (bond, background checks, local license, possible 2-acre rule) but has a clearer state-level process and a longer license term.
