## 1. Stormwater: EPA MSGP Sector M (MA) and RIPDES MSGP (RI)

### Takeaway
In Massachusetts EPA Region 1 (not MassDEP) issues the industrial stormwater permit; the 2021 MSGP expired 28 Feb 2026 without a replacement, is administratively continued for existing permittees, and a **new** yard opening in MA today cannot get normal coverage — it must operate under EPA's 27 Feb 2026 "No Action Assurance" (email an NOI form, comply with the full 2021 MSGP). Rhode Island runs its own program: RIDEM's 2024 RIPDES MSGP (effective 1 Sep 2024) with Sector M-specific additions (oil & grease benchmark, copper indicator monitoring), and a new facility must file an NOI 60 days before discharging.

### Cited Findings

**Who is covered / what the permit requires (federal)**
- [V] Sector M covers automobile salvage yards, primary SIC 5015, including "battery reclaimers, salvage yards, and automobile recyclers"; both dismantling used vehicles to sell parts and wholesale/retail distribution of used parts require industrial stormwater permit coverage. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet, EPA-833-F-06-028, Feb 2021](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [V] Common requirements: written SWPPP, implementation of control measures, NOI submission; SWPPP kept on site and updated; visual, analytical and/or compliance monitoring. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [V] Since 1995 the MSGP has required: SWPPP; stormwater control measures; inspections; analytical monitoring; corrective action; submission of monitoring data and annual reports. The NOI must also certify eligibility on endangered species and historic properties. The 2021 MSGP covers about 2,200 facilities nationally. — [EPA OECA No Action Assurance memo, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/oeca-no-action-assurance-memo-msgp.pdf)
- [V] The 2021 MSGP applies in Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Mexico, DC, most territories, most Indian Country, and certain federal facilities (i.e., EPA is the permitting authority in MA). — [EPA OECA memo, footnote 1](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/oeca-no-action-assurance-memo-msgp.pdf)
- [V] Pollutants EPA associates with salvage yards: oil and grease, ethylene glycol, heavy metals, mercury, sulfuric acid, petroleum hydrocarbons, TSS, arsenic, chlorinated solvents, detergents, phosphorus; a note flags PFAS as an additional possible source. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet, Table 1](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [V] In enforcement releases EPA lists salvage-yard pollutants of concern as TSS, metals (iron, mercury, lead and others), chemical oxygen demand, PCBs, and oils/fluids. — [EPA press release, LKQ Northeast, Mar 2021](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/massachusetts-auto-salvage-company-settles-alleged-clean-water-act-violations)
- [V] Iron benchmark was eliminated in the 2021 EPA MSGP (RIDEM removed its iron benchmark for subsector M1 and others "to be consistent with the 2021 EPA MSGP", citing lack of documented toxicity per the NRC study). So the "TSS, aluminum, iron, lead" list in older guidance is out of date: iron is no longer a benchmark. — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP renewal training, 20 Aug 2024](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf)

**Status of the 2021 MSGP and the 2026 reissuance (critical for a new MA entrant)**
- [V] The 2021 MSGP was issued 15 Jan 2021 and expired at 11:59 pm ET on 28 Feb 2026. The proposed next MSGP was published 13 Dec 2024 (89 Fed. Reg. 101000); comment period extended and closed 19 May 2025; EPA received 90 comment letters. — [EPA OECA memo](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/oeca-no-action-assurance-memo-msgp.pdf)
- [V] EPA said it could not finalize before expiry; it is "taking more time to re-engage with stakeholders and consider next steps", and ESA consultation plus CWA §401 certification "alone can take six months to a year". — [EPA OECA memo](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/oeca-no-action-assurance-memo-msgp.pdf)
- [V] Existing permittees: coverage is administratively continued (APA §558(c), 40 CFR 122.6) and they must keep complying with the 2021 MSGP. EPA's page (last updated 1 Mar 2026 when read) still links only to the "Proposed 2026 MSGP". — [EPA, Stormwater Discharges from Industrial Activities](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/stormwater-discharges-industrial-activities)
- [V] New facilities that start discharging after 28 Feb 2026 "will not be able to obtain general permit coverage until a new permit is issued." EPA will not pursue civil/administrative enforcement for unpermitted discharge if the new operator: (1) meets 2021 MSGP eligibility (Part 1); (2) **before discharging**, emails the NOI form (Appendix G) to msgp@epa.gov — which "does not constitute permit coverage"; (3) complies with all applicable 2021 MSGP requirements — SWPPP (Part 6), control measures (Part 2), inspections (Part 3), effluent-limit/impaired-waters/indicator/benchmark monitoring (Part 4), corrective action (Part 5), sector-specific requirements (Part 8), state-specific conditions (Part 9), recordkeeping (Part 7). — [EPA OECA memo](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/oeca-no-action-assurance-memo-msgp.pdf)
- [V] The assurance does **not** cover existing facilities that were discharging before 28 Feb 2026 without coverage. Facilities starting after the next MSGP's effective date must file an NOI under the new permit. EPA expected about 90 new facilities nationally to rely on the assurance. — [EPA OECA memo](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/oeca-no-action-assurance-memo-msgp.pdf)
- [S] Once the new MSGP takes effect, continuing dischargers are expected to file a new NOI within 90 days of the effective date (per the proposed permit, as summarized by trade press). — [TRC, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.trccompanies.com/?p=33553); proposal text at [EPA proposed 2026 MSGP Parts 1–7](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-12/proposed-2026-msgp-permit-parts-1-7.pdf)
- [S] Two searches in Nov 2026 (one extended) found no Federal Register notice of a final 2026 MSGP and no effective date; a trade article states nobody knows when finalization arrives. — [Federal Register proposal notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/13/2024-29402/national-pollutant-discharge-elimination-system-npdes-2026-issuance-of-the-multi-sector-general); [Environment+Energy Leader](https://www.environmentenergyleader.com/stories/industrial-stormwater-permit-gap-creates-compliance-risk-this-summer,127002)

**No-exposure exclusion**
- [V] A facility that prevents exposure of ALL relevant activities and materials to precipitation may claim no exposure and qualify for a waiver; a regulated facility must either get permit coverage or submit a no exposure certification. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet, p. 8](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [V] Rhode Island: No Exposure Certifications are filed through NeT-MSGP and expire every 5 years; permit coverage must be terminated before filing an NEC. A separate optional "No Discharge Certification" does not expire. — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP training](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf)

**Rhode Island RIPDES MSGP (permit RIR500000)**
- [V] RIDEM reissued the MSGP on 16 Jul 2024, effective 1 Sep 2024; the 2019 MSGP had expired 2 May 2024. — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP training](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf); permit text at [EPA-hosted final RIR500000 (2024)](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-06/final-rir500000-multi-sector-general-permit-stormwater-industrial-2024.pdf)
- [V] New facilities must submit the NOI **60 days prior** to commencing industrial stormwater discharge. NOI is filed electronically in EPA's NeT-MSGP on CDX (Login.gov account required; the signatory must meet 40 CFR 122.22(a)(1)); the Stormwater Management Plan (SWMP) must be uploaded with the NOI; a six-digit NAICS code is required, but SIC codes still determine sector requirements. — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP training](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf)
- [V] Ongoing obligations: site-specific SWMP; outfall monitoring (benchmark, impairment, ELG, indicator); **quarterly** routine inspections; **quarterly** visual assessments; tiered corrective actions (Levels 1–3); **annual report due 30 January**; DMRs via NetDMR (semi-annual benchmark monitoring; first DMR under the 2024 permit was due 15 Jul 2025 for the Jan–Jun 2025 period); records kept **5 years** (up from 3). — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP training](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf)
- [V] Sector M-specific changes in 2024: **oil & grease benchmark monitoring added** for Sector M; **copper indicator (monitor-only) monitoring** added for Sector M; iron benchmark removed for subsector M1. — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP training](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf)
- [V] Other 2024 additions applying to all sectors: PFAS evaluation (identify PFAS-containing materials in the SWMP, evaluate alternatives, implement BMPs); major-storm-event resilience measures; antecedent dry period for sampling cut from 72 to 48 hours; monitoring required even if the qualifying storm falls outside business hours (if safe); automatic permit transfer eliminated (seller terminates, buyer files new NOI). — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP training](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf)
- [V] RIDEM compliance monitoring: "annual random or targeted inspections and audits" plus review of DMRs, annual reports and corrective action reports. — [RIDEM 2024 MSGP training](https://dem.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur861/files/2024-08/msgp2024-renewal-training-pres.pdf)

### Inferences
- For the Waltham owner, a new MA yard in late 2026 sits in a legal grey zone: it gets enforcement discretion from EPA, not a permit. A No Action Assurance binds EPA only; it does not obviously shield against Clean Water Act citizen suits or state Attorney General actions, both of which are active in MA (see section 9). This is a material risk to flag and to confirm with counsel.
- Buying an existing yard that already holds 2021 MSGP coverage (administratively continued) is cleaner on stormwater than a greenfield start, but EPA's memo expressly gives no relief to existing yards that never had coverage — a due-diligence item (check the yard's NOI/permit ID in EPA's eNOI/ECHO records).
- A fully indoor dismantling operation with no outdoor vehicle/parts storage could claim no-exposure, but a conventional yard with outdoor vehicle rows cannot, because ALL industrial materials and activities must be sheltered.
- RI is procedurally more predictable right now (valid permit through its term, 60-day pre-filing), but its Sector M monitoring list is broader (oil & grease, copper).

### Gaps
- **Benchmark values**: I could not open the 2021 MSGP Part 8 Sector M table. From background knowledge (unverified here): Sector M benchmarks under the 2021 MSGP are TSS 100 mg/L, total aluminum 1.1 mg/L, and hardness-dependent total lead; quarterly benchmark monitoring in years 1 and 4; quarterly visual assessments; quarterly routine inspections; annual report by 30 January; Additional Implementation Measures (AIM) tiers on exceedance. Verify against Part 4 and Part 8.M of the 2021 MSGP.
- **Fees**: I found no source for NOI fees. Background knowledge (unverified): EPA charges no fee for MSGP NOIs; RI fee status unknown. Massachusetts-specific Part 9 conditions of the MSGP (state 401 certification conditions) were not reviewed.
- **Final 2026 MSGP**: not found as of 24 Nov 2026; EPA's own page was last updated 1 Mar 2026 when read. It is possible a final permit issued very recently and was not indexed — re-check epa.gov/npdes/stormwater-discharges-industrial-activities before acting.
- What the proposed 2026 MSGP would change for Sector M (e.g., PFAS indicator monitoring, AIM changes) was not reviewed.
- Typical consultant cost for a SWPPP and annual sampling lab costs: no source found.
- RI numeric benchmark values for Sector M (TSS, aluminum, lead, oil & grease) not extracted; they are in the RIR500000 permit text linked above.

## 2. Oil: SPCC, used/waste oil, tanks

### Takeaway
A yard with more than 1,320 gallons of aboveground oil capacity (counting containers of 55 gallons and up) needs a federal SPCC plan, which can be self-certified up to 10,000 gallons; EPA Region 1 has penalized New England salvage yards specifically for SPCC failures. Massachusetts is stricter than federal on used oil: waste oil is a regulated hazardous waste under 310 CMR 30.250–30.256 with its own generator status.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Everett's Auto Parts (Brockton/East Bridgewater, MA) was cited for failing to fully implement its SPCC plan, "including inadequate secondary containment for storage tanks"; the Oil Pollution Prevention regulation applied because of its aboveground oil tank capacity. — [EPA press release, May 2024](https://epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-fines-brockton-mass-company-alleged-clean-water-act-violations)
- [S] SPCC "qualified facility" rules: total aboveground oil storage of 10,000 gallons or less allows the owner to self-certify the plan instead of using a Professional Engineer; Tier I additionally requires no single container over 5,000 gallons and allows use of EPA's template; eligibility requires no single discharge over 1,000 gallons and no two discharges over 42 gallons each in a 12-month period in the prior three years; the plan is not filed with EPA; exceeding 10,000 gallons later requires PE certification within 6 months. — [EPA, Is My Facility a Qualified Facility under the SPCC Rule?](https://epa.gov/oil-spills-prevention-and-preparedness-regulations/my-facility-qualified-facility-under-spcc-rule); [EPA, Tier I vs Tier II](https://epa.gov/oil-spills-prevention-and-preparedness-regulations/difference-between-spcc-tier-i-and-tier-ii); [40 CFR 112.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/112.6)
- [S] The 1,320-gallon aboveground applicability threshold is referenced by a fire-agency summary of the SPCC tiers (≥1,320 and ≤10,000 gallons). — [LAFD APSA Tier Information](https://www.lafd.org/apsa-tier-information)
- [S] "Waste oil is a regulated hazardous waste in Massachusetts"; requirements are at 310 CMR 30.250–30.256. Generator status is determined separately for hazardous waste and for waste oil. — [MassDEP, Waste Oil Management](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/waste-oil-management); [310 CMR 30.252](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/310-CMR-30-252)
- [S] Waste oil manifested in MA under code MA01 is subject to the MA hazardous waste transporter fee, paid by the licensed transporter rather than the generator (2005 guidance; may be dated). — [MassDEP Guidance 2005-01](https://www.mass.gov/doc/guidance-document-2005-01-applicability-of-the-massachusetts-hazardous-waste-transporter-fee/download)
- [S] A MA VSQG shipping waste oil to another site that burns it must keep transport logs (dates, quantities, receiving location name/address/generator number, signatures). — [MassDEP MassCAR Waste Oil Management](https://www.mass.gov/doc/masscar-waste-oil-management/download)
- [V] EPA Sector M BMPs for oil: drain used motor oil into double-walled tanks; re-use on site or send for re-refining/fuel blending; drain oil filters for 24 hours and send to scrap; "do not burn used oil unless approved"; never use fluids, oil or fuel for dust or weed control; tank storage secured and locked; secondary containment (diking/berming) with valves kept closed and locked. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet, Table 2](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [S] Rhode Island has its own used oil management standards within the hazardous waste rules (250-RICR-140-10-1 §1.16), applying in place of 40 CFR 279 except where noted. — [RI Rules and Regulations for Hazardous Waste Management](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/Regulations/part/250-140-10-1); [RIDEM Hazardous Waste and Used Oil Topics](https://dem.ri.gov/programs/wastemanagement/facilities/hazardous-waste-oil.php)
- [S] RIDEM NOVs at auto businesses commonly cite an open aboveground used oil tank and containers not labeled "Used Oil". — [RIDEM enforcement summaries, e.g. Feb 2025](https://dem.ri.gov/february-2025-enforcement-action-summary)
- [S] Abandoned tanks are an acquisition risk: MassDEP penalized a recycling company over three underground tanks (gasoline and waste oil) abandoned in place since 2005 at a Northbridge auto salvage property. — [MassDEP press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/recycling-company-penalized-for-violating-waste-site-cleanup-regulations-at-their-northbridge-property)
- [S] MA UST systems are regulated under 310 CMR 80.00. — [310 CMR 80.00](https://www.mass.gov/doc/310-cmr-8000-underground-storage-tank-ust-systems/download)

### Inferences
- A yard draining even 1,000–2,000 vehicles per year will usually exceed 1,320 gallons of aggregate capacity (used oil tank + gasoline/diesel recovery tanks + antifreeze is not oil, but hydraulic/transmission fluid is), so an SPCC plan should be assumed. Tier I self-certification keeps this cheap if total capacity stays at or below 10,000 gallons.
- In MA, used oil cannot be handled under the relaxed federal Part 279 model; it counts toward a waste-oil generator status and ships on a hazardous waste manifest with a licensed transporter.

### Gaps
- Exact text of 40 CFR 112.1(d)(2)(ii) (1,320 gal aboveground; 42,000 gal completely buried) was not opened; the 1,320 figure is supported only by a secondary source here.
- MA aboveground tank rules (fire code permits for tanks, 502 CMR 5.00 for ASTs over 10,000 gallons) and used-oil space heater rules (310 CMR 30.222 / air rules): not researched; background knowledge only.
- RI UST/AST rules (250-RICR-140-25-1; RI Oil Pollution Control Regulations) not researched.
- MA waste-oil generator thresholds and accumulation limits not confirmed from the regulation text.

## 3. Hazardous waste generator status and specific waste streams

### Takeaway
Both states require a generator ID/registration and status tracking; a well-run yard that recycles fluids typically aims to stay a Very Small Quantity Generator. The stream-by-stream rules (mercury switches, refrigerant, airbags, batteries, tires, EV batteries) are where salvage yards differ from a used-car lot, and several carry stand-alone federal or state mandates.

### Cited Findings

**Generator status**
- [S] MA: any shop generating hazardous waste must register with MassDEP; VSQGs and SQGs submit a Hazardous Waste Generator Registration Form. A VSQG generates less than 100 kg/month (and no acutely hazardous waste) and may accumulate up to 2,200 lb (about 270 gallons) indefinitely; MassDEP summaries also note a VSQG should not self-transport more than 55 gallons at a time. Full generator rules: 310 CMR 30.341–30.353. — [MassDEP Hazardous Waste Management](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/hazardous-waste-management); [MassDEP VSQG summary](https://www.mass.gov/doc/summary-of-very-small-quantity-generator-vsqg-requirements/download); [MassDEP SQG summary](https://www.mass.gov/doc/summary-of-small-quantity-generator-sqg-requirements/download); [Registration requirements and fees](https://www.mass.gov/doc/registration-requirements-and-fees-for-generators-of-hazardous-waste/download)
- [S] RI: generator rules are at 250-RICR-140-10-1.7; categories are LQG, SQG and Conditionally Exempt SQG (CESQG) as defined in §1.5. — [RI Hazardous Waste Regulations](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/Regulations/part/250-140-10-1)
- [S] Federal reference thresholds: VSQG up to 100 kg/month non-acute (1 kg acute); SQG up to 1,000 kg/month. — [EPA hazardous waste generator FAQ (archived)](https://19january2025snapshot.epa.gov/hwgenerators/frequent-questions-about-hazardous-waste-generation)

**Fluids, filters, batteries, lead**
- [V] EPA BMPs: drain all fluids on arrival; segregate; recycle antifreeze, gasoline, used oil, mineral spirits, washer fluid, solvents; keep waste streams separate (e.g., waste oil vs mineral spirits); "good" fuel can be reused on site, "bad" fuel must be disposed; remove batteries as soon as feasible, store on impervious surface, indoors on a pallet or outdoors in a leak-proof container, cracked batteries in secondary containment; lead parts (wheel weights) in a covered container. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet, Table 2](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [S] MA antifreeze: may not be landfilled, discharged to ground/catch basins, or sewered without a permit; spent antifreeze kept segregated is presumed non-hazardous and ships on a bill of lading rather than a manifest. — [MassDEP MassCAR Antifreeze Management and Recycling](https://www.mass.gov/doc/masscar-antifreeze-management-and-recycling/download)
- [S] MA oil filters: drain at least 12 hours or crush, otherwise manage as hazardous waste; used **fuel** filters are ignitable and toxic and should always be managed as hazardous waste. — [MassDEP waste oil / used oil filter management](https://www.mass.gov/doc/automotive-service-repair-waste-oil-used-oil-filter-management/download); [MassDEP, Common Hazardous Wastes in Auto Shops](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/common-hazardous-wastes-in-auto-shops)
- [S] MA lead-acid batteries: contain corrosive/toxic materials that should be managed as hazardous waste if not properly recycled; store non-leaking, on pallets. — [MassDEP, Common Hazardous Wastes in Auto Shops](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/common-hazardous-wastes-in-auto-shops)

**Mercury switches (MA)**
- [S] 310 CMR 74.00 (under M.G.L. c. 21H §6C, the Mercury Management Act of 2006) applies to vehicle recyclers, scrap recyclers accepting end-of-life vehicles, dealers and manufacturers: no person may crush, or arrange to crush, an end-of-life vehicle without first removing mercury-added components including switches; covers partial crushing, flattening, baling, and mobile crushers. Switches inaccessible due to significant damage are exempt (310 CMR 74.04(2)). — [310 CMR 74.04](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/310-CMR-74-04); [M.G.L. c. 21H §6C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleII/Chapter21H/Section6C); [MassDEP vehicle recycler mercury fact sheet](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/08/ps/vrhgfact.pdf)
- [S] Removed switches must be recycled as Universal Waste or disposed as hazardous waste and may not be resold; the statute targets a 90% switch capture rate; regulation last amended Aug 2009. — [MassDEP 310 CMR 74.00 page](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/310-CMR-7400-removal-and-recycling-of-mercury-added-components-in-vehicles); [M.G.L. c. 21H §6C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleII/Chapter21H/Section6C)
- [S] MassDEP has an annual vehicle recycler / mobile crusher mercury certification form (2024 reporting year seen), and standard bill-of-lading certification language that switches were removed. — [MassDEP Vehicle Recycler Mobile Crusher Certification](https://www.mass.gov/doc/vehicle-recycler-mobile-crusher-certification/download); [MassDEP Mercury Forms & Guidance](https://www.mass.gov/lists/massdep-mercury-forms-instructions-guidance)
- [S] ELVS (End of Life Vehicle Solutions, the automaker program) supplies collection buckets and pays shipping and recycling; an ELVS notice says MA recyclers receive **$3 per switch** from Sept 2008 if they certify the switches came from vehicles salvaged in MA. — [ELVS](https://elvsolutions.org/?p=300)
- [V] EPA BMP: remove mercury switches as soon as possible, ship to ELVS; store in covered leak-proof containers labeled "Hazardous Waste – Spent Mercury Switches". — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)

**Airbags**
- [S] EPA's interim final rule "Safe Management of Recalled Airbags" (83 FR 61552, effective 30 Nov 2018) added 40 CFR 261.4(j): airbag waste (modules and inflators) is conditionally exempt from hazardous waste requirements at the "airbag waste handler" (e.g., dealership, salvage yard) and during transport to an airbag waste collection facility or designated facility, where it is again managed as RCRA hazardous waste. — [83 FR 61552](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2018-11-30/html/2018-25892.htm); [EPA airbag waste FAQ](https://www.epa.gov/node/227307)
- [V] EPA BMP: "Remove or deploy airbags prior to crushing." — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)

**Refrigerants (MVAC)**
- [S] 40 CFR Part 82 Subpart F defines "motor vehicle disposal facility" to include automotive recycling facilities, scrap yards and salvage yards that dismantle, crush or recycle MVACs. Safe-disposal rule is 40 CFR 82.155. — [eCFR 40 CFR 82.155](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-82/subpart-F/section-82.155)
- [S] The final person in the disposal chain must either recover refrigerant or obtain a signed statement from the supplier that refrigerant was properly recovered; recovery equipment must be certified/approved for MVACs or small appliances; people recovering refrigerant from MVACs solely for disposal are **not** required to be certified technicians. — [EPA guide for local governments on appliance disposal](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2021-05/documents/what_local_goverments_need_to_know_about_appliance_disposal.pdf); [EPA stationary refrigeration safe disposal requirements (archived)](https://19january2021snapshot.epa.gov/section608/stationary-refrigeration-safe-disposal-requirements_.html)

**Tires**
- [S] MA: a pile of whole or shredded tires is treated as a "dumping ground" requiring site assignment from the local Board of Health; outdoor tire piles over **2,500 cubic feet** required a permit from the local fire official under former 527 CMR 10.03(5); indoor storage should follow NFPA 230 and 780 CMR. Note 527 CMR 10.00 has since been repealed and folded into 527 CMR 1.00, so the citation is outdated. — [MassDEP waste tire management guidance](https://www.mass.gov/doc/automotive-service-repair-waste-tire-management/download)
- [S] RI: no unlicensed person may store more than **400 used vehicle tires** at any location (R.I.G.L. §23-63-2; license under §23-63-4); licensed tire storage facilities need a contingency plan and financial surety/insurance covering the worst-case cleanup. — [R.I.G.L. §23-63-2](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE23/23-63/23-63-2.htm)
- [V] EPA BMP: do not stockpile old tires (fire hazard, mosquito/rodent breeding); use indoor tire racks. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)

**EV / hybrid high-voltage batteries**
- [S] DOT/PHMSA: damaged or defective lithium batteries face extra packaging restrictions under 49 CFR 173.185(f) (individual non-metallic inner packaging, surrounded by non-combustible, non-conductive cushioning, etc.); where a battery installed in a vehicle is damaged or defective, it must be removed and shipped under 173.185(f) unless PHMSA approves otherwise. — [PHMSA Safety Advisory, Transportation of EVs / Lithium Batteries, Apr 2023](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/sites/phmsa.dot.gov/files/2023-04/PHMSA-Safety-Advisory-Transportation-of-EVs-Lithium-Batteries-April-2023.pdf)
- [S] Damaged batteries can ignite days to weeks after damage; NHTSA guidance for tow/storage lots says to keep monitoring for leaking fluid, sparks, smoke, or gurgling/bubbling sounds. — [NHTSA Interim Guidance for EV/HEV HV batteries — towing, recovery, storage](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.dot.gov/files/811576-interimguidehev-hv-batt_towing-recovery-storage-v2.pdf)
- [S] Insurer guidance: locate EV battery storage away from primary buildings, sprinkler it, keep piled storage at least 10 ft from other combustibles. — [Zurich EV battery storage loss prevention bulletin](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/zurichinsura267-zwpzna-prod-c557/media/project/zurich-headless/zna/docs/kh/auto/ev-battery-storage-loss-prevention-bulletin.pdf)

**Other**
- [V] Asbestos brake shoes/clutches, if handled, should be wetted to prevent airborne particulates. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [V] PCBs appear on EPA's list of stormwater pollutants of concern at salvage yards. — [EPA LKQ release](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/massachusetts-auto-salvage-company-settles-alleged-clean-water-act-violations)

### Inferences
- The MA mercury rule is a hard gate on crushing: every hulk sold to a shredder/scrap processor effectively needs switch-removal certification on the bill of lading, plus an annual certification to MassDEP.
- Refrigerant: buying one certified MVAC recovery machine and keeping recovery logs/verification statements is the low-cost path; the yard cannot simply cut A/C lines.
- EV batteries are the fastest-changing risk area and the least codified for salvage yards; expect the local fire chief and the insurer, rather than MassDEP, to set practical conditions (isolation distance, quarantine area).

### Gaps

## 5. Air, fire, wetlands, groundwater discharge, floor drains

### Takeaway
Floor drains are the most clear-cut item: in MA, drains in vehicle maintenance/dismantling areas may only go to a municipal sewer (with approval) or an approved industrial wastewater holding (tight) tank — discharge to the ground is an illegal Class V injection well under 310 CMR 27.00. Fire-code permits (tires, flammables, hot work) are issued locally under 527 CMR 1.00. I found little salvage-specific material on air permits or wetlands.

### Cited Findings
- [S] MassDEP: "all floor drains must only discharge to municipal sewers or approved industrial wastewater holding tanks"; shops holding wastewater in a tank submit a one-time compliance certification to MassDEP under 310 CMR 18.00 before hauling it off site. — [MassDEP, Wastewater Regulations and Best Practices (MassCAR)](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/wastewater-regulations-and-best-practices)
- [S] Floor drain discharges to the ground are UIC Class V wells (defined at 310 CMR 27.05), administered by MassDEP's Drinking Water Program, and must be closed. — [MassDEP UIC closure FAQs](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/faqs-massachusetts-closure-guidance-for-underground-injection-control-uic-wells-including-shallow-injection-wells)
- [S] In wellhead protection areas (Zone II), 310 CMR 22.21(2)(a)(8) prohibits floor drainage to the ground in hazardous-material areas and is stricter than the plumbing code (248 CMR 10.00); vehicle repair shops must also have oil/water separators; many municipalities have adopted MassDEP's model Board of Health floor drain regulation, which is broader. — [MassDEP Wellhead Protection Guide — Floor Drain Discharges](https://www.mass.gov/guides/wellhead-protection-guide-floor-drain-discharges); [Model BOH floor drain regulation](https://www.mass.gov/doc/model-board-of-health-floor-drain-regulation/download)
- [V] EPA BMPs: plug floor drains connected to storm or sanitary sewer or of unknown destination, or install a sump that is pumped regularly; dye-test for cross-connections; no hosing down shop floors (dry cleanup); contain steam-cleaning/wash water or discharge only under an NPDES permit. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [S] Enforcement example on wastewater: MassDEP penalized 182 MTR LLC for wastewater (septage disposal) violations at its Highway Auto Salvage facility in Northampton. — [MassDEP press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/massdep-penalizes-182-mtr-llc-for-wastewater-violations-at-its-highway-auto-salvage-facility-in-northampton)
- [S] MA fire code is 527 CMR 1.00, the Massachusetts Comprehensive Fire Safety Code (2021 NFPA 1-based edition effective 3 Feb 2023 was the latest edition found); garages and motor-fuel storage were historically under 527 CMR 5.00. — [527 CMR 1.00 (2021 edition)](https://www.mass.gov/doc/massachusetts-527-cmr-100-2021-edition-effective-february-3-2023/download)
- [S] Outdoor tire piles above 2,500 cubic feet require a local fire official permit (older citation 527 CMR 10.03(5), now repealed/recodified). — [MassDEP waste tire guidance](https://www.mass.gov/doc/automotive-service-repair-waste-tire-management/download)
- [V] Vehicle crushing BMPs: capture crusher fluids in a covered spill-proof container; ideal foundation is engineering fabric plus gravel or a bermed impermeable concrete surface; be certain all fluids are drained before crushing. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [S] Scrap/salvage operations in MA have faced AG claims for dust and noise pollution in addition to stormwater (Brayton Point scrap facility, Somerset, settled Apr 2023). — [MA AG press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/ags-office-settles-claims-of-illegal-dust-and-noise-pollution-and-stormwater-discharge-against-owner-and-operators-of-somersets-brayton-point-scrap-facility)

### Inferences
- A used-car dealership building in Waltham with existing floor drains must confirm where they discharge before any dismantling starts; conversion to a tight tank or sewer connection with oil/water separator is a likely up-front cost.
- Dust/noise from crushing and torch cutting is regulated in MA mainly as nuisance under 310 CMR 7.00 (see Gaps), and the Somerset case shows it is enforced against metal recyclers.

### Gaps
- Air: no source found on MA air plan approval thresholds for crushers/shredders or torch cutting, or on RI air permits for salvage yards. Background knowledge (unverified): 310 CMR 7.09 (dust/odor), 7.10 (noise), and 7.02 plan approval thresholds apply generically; car crushers alone rarely need plan approval.
- Fire: hot work (cutting/welding) permits, flammable/combustible liquid storage permits, and the M.G.L. c.148 §13 land license for storing flammables were not confirmed by any source found. Background knowledge (unverified): 527 CMR 1.00 Chapter 41 requires hot-work permits/training, and c.148 §13 licenses are issued by the local licensing authority with annual registration — confirm with Waltham Fire Prevention.
- Wetlands: no salvage-specific source found. Background knowledge (unverified): MA Wetlands Protection Act (M.G.L. c.131 §40, 310 CMR 10.00) requires Conservation Commission review for work within 100 ft of wetlands / 200 ft of perennial rivers; RI Freshwater Wetlands rules (250-RICR-150-15-3) apply similarly.
- Groundwater discharge permits (314 CMR 5.00) and sewer connection/industrial user permits (MWRA for Waltham): not researched.
- RI floor drain/UIC rules (RIDEM UIC program, 250-RICR-150-05-4): not researched.
- RI fire code requirements: not researched.

## 7. Best-practice infrastructure and rough costs

### Takeaway
Regulators converge on the same physical kit: a covered, bermed, impermeable concrete dismantling/draining pad; a fluid evacuation rack with segregated double-walled tanks; secondary containment; indoor/covered battery and parts storage; and an oil/water separator where stormwater is treated. I could not find sourced dollar figures for any of it.

### Cited Findings
- [V] EPA: install "a consolidated processing area, including a covered and bermed impermeable concrete surface equipped with a drain, where all fluids are drained"; drain fluids on arrival; inspect vehicles for leaks on arrival and quarterly; use catch sleds/drip pans when pulling parts in the yard; cover storage areas with roofs or tarps; berms at the property line against run-on; filtering devices and oil/water separators; detention ponds; process hulks for shredding promptly once stripped. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet, Table 2 and p. 4](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [V] EPA notes treatment BMPs (oil-water separators, wet ponds, proprietary filters) "are generally more expensive to install and maintain" than prevention BMPs, which should be considered first. — [EPA Sector M Fact Sheet, p. 3](https://www.epa.gov/sites/production/files/2015-10/documents/sector_m_autosalvage.pdf)
- [S] MassDEP: a concrete pad can be coated with epoxy resin so fluids do not drain through. — [MassDEP vehicle crushing BMP](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/08/sg/vehcrush.pdf)
- [S] Commercial fluid-removal racks (e.g., Iron Ax "Enviro Rack") are self-contained, air-powered, about 7,000 lb, and can sit on a slab or on the ground; some models route catch basins to an integrated oil/antifreeze separator; pricing is by quote. — [Enviro Rack product page](https://www.environmental-expert.com/products/enviro-racks-121199)
- [S] MassDEP MassCAR promotes vehicle fluid evacuation caddies for shops. — [MassDEP, Vehicle Fluid Evacuation Caddies](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/vehicle-fluid-evacuation-caddies)
- [S] Other states' guidance is consistent: drain over a covered concrete drip pad with spill containment (WA); tank secondary containment sized to at least 110% of tank contents (NJ); roofed concrete draining/dismantling area (TN). — [WA Ecology, Auto recyclers](https://ecology.wa.gov/Regulations-Permits/Guidance-technical-assistance/Dangerous-waste-guidance/Common-dangerous-waste/Auto-recyclers); [TN SBEAP stormwater guide](https://www.tennessee.gov/content/dam/tn/environment/small-business/documents/sbeap_stormwater_2ownersfinal.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because all exposed activity triggers MSGP coverage and monitoring, capital spent on roofing the dismantling area and core/engine storage directly reduces benchmark exceedance risk (and thus corrective-action and enforcement cost).
- The most cost-effective sequence is prevention first (indoor draining, covered storage, drip control), with engineered treatment only if benchmark sampling shows exceedances — this is also the sequence EPA's corrective-action framework expects.

### Gaps
- **No sourced cost data** was found for: fluid evacuation racks, concrete pads, oil/water separators, canopies, SWPPP preparation, stormwater sampling, SPCC plan preparation, or annual hazardous-waste hauling. The report writer should treat any cost figure as needing vendor/consultant quotes. (Background, unverified and not for citation: commercial evacuation racks are commonly quoted in the tens of thousands of dollars; SWPPP preparation by a consultant commonly a few thousand to low tens of thousands; these are impressions, not data.)
- ARA CAR program requirements and audit costs were not researched.
