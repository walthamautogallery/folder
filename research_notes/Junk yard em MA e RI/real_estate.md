## 1. Land needs: acreage by business model, cars per acre, site features

### Takeaway
There is no published standard for cars per acre; real-world examples run from roughly 20 to 80 vehicles per acre at ground level, and self-service yards need far more land than full-service/dismantle-and-shelve operations. In Rhode Island, a state-licensed yard must be at least 2 contiguous acres with a 6-ft screening fence and highway setbacks unless the town issues its own local license; New England yards actually listed for sale run about 4 to 34 acres.

### Cited Findings
- An American Planning Association (PAS) report on junked cars put the average auto wrecking yard at 8 acres, about 2 acres in heavily populated areas, and up to 30 acres where land is cheap; it notes yards that let customers roam for parts need more land. This is an old (1960s-70s era) report, so use as rough orientation only (search excerpt, not opened). — [APA PAS Report 201](https://planning.org/pas/reports/report201.htm)
- Self-service example: a Detroit self-serve yard reportedly holds about 2,000 vehicles on 25 acres (derived: ~80 vehicles/acre) (search excerpt, not opened). — [FenderBender](https://fenderbender.com/running-a-shop/finance/article/33033698/new-salvage-yard-not-likely-to-lose-itself-in-the-moment)
- Self-service example: a Florida U-Pull-It grew its main yard to 49 acres, keeps roughly 1,000 cars on site, and paved the entire site in concrete for customer safety and traversability (derived: ~20 vehicles/acre over gross acreage; the source does not say how much of the 49 acres is car storage) (search excerpt, not opened). — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/article/garden-street-u-pull-it-florida-profile)
- Vertical racking can roughly double capacity: an industry vendor says a yard storing 2,000 vehicles in single-level rows may hold more than 4,000 after rack installation (vendor claim, search excerpt, not opened). — [Hub Industrial](https://www.hubindustrial.com/resources/auto-recycling-news-and-resources/scaling-an-auto-recycling-business-without-adding-yard-space.html)
- Rhode Island physical requirements (apply when the city/town does NOT issue its own local junk/second-hand license): more than 1,000 ft from the nearest edge of any interstate/primary highway; more than 600 ft from any other state highway; more than 300 ft from any park, bathing beach, playground, school, church or cemetery and not in view of them; screened and enclosed by a maintained fence at least 6 ft high (unless a natural barrier screens it); at least 2 acres in one continuous lot; survey/assessor map required. If the town issues a local license, these specific physical requirements do not apply; if the town denied the local license, DBR will not license the site. — [RI DBR Auto Wrecking and Salvage Yards FAQ](https://dbr.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- RI applicants must first obtain a letter from the local zoning official stating auto salvage is allowed on the property; DBR license fee is $750 per location plus a $10,000 surety bond; the application asks about RIDEM compliance. — [RI DBR FAQ](https://dbr.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- Massachusetts: before granting a Class 3 license the local licensing board must be satisfied the applicant "has available a place of business suitable for the purpose"; the license must list all premises occupied, and permits for a change of location or additions to the premises are granted separately. — [MGL c.140 s.59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Actual New England yard sizes from listings (see section 3 for prices): Berlin MA ~7.5 acres in salvage use on a ~25-acre parcel; Worcester-area yard with ~13 acres of concrete surface; North Providence RI 6+ acres with 700 ft frontage; Westerly RI 4+ acres; Plaistow NH/Haverhill MA 34+ acres; a Rochester NY yard with ~4 fenced acres on a 4.94-acre parcel. — [LoopNet Berlin](https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/76-Coburn-Rd-Berlin-MA/39054516/); [Crexi 267 Granite St](https://www.crexi.com/properties/249309/massachusetts-267-granite-st); [Lila Delman, N. Providence](https://liladelman.com/?p=5744742); [Coldwell Banker, Westerly](https://www.coldwellbankerhomes.com/ri/westerly/129-oak-st/pid_59302048/); [Crexi Brandy Brow](https://www.crexi.com/properties/2167003/new-hampshire-brandy-brow-auto-parts); [LoopNet Rochester](https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/522-Trolley-Blvd-Rochester-NY/40768031/)
- Stormwater from auto salvage yards carries oil, heavy metals, acids and other industrial compounds, and EPA has brought Clean Water Act enforcement against a Massachusetts auto salvage company; this is why paving, drainage and containment are site-selection issues, not afterthoughts (search excerpts, not opened). — [EPA news release](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/massachusetts-auto-salvage-company-settles-alleged-clean-water-act-violations)

### Inferences
- A working planning range of ~20-80 vehicles per gross acre (single level) follows from the two self-service examples; it is not an industry standard. On that basis a 1-3 acre full-service/dismantling yard would hold on the order of 50-250 vehicles at ground level (more with racking), and a self-service yard with 1,000+ cars points to 15-25+ acres.
- The task's framing (small full-service 1-3 acres; self-service 10-20+ acres) is consistent with the APA "2 acres in populated areas / 8 average / 30 where cheap" figures and with the 4-13 acre size of the New England yards actually on the market.
- Inside Route 128, a parcel of even 2-3 acres with the right zoning is the binding constraint, which pushes a Waltham-based operator toward I-495, Worcester County, the South Coast, or Rhode Island (supported by the Prescott IOS commentary in section 2).
- In RI, towns that issue their own local junk license effectively replace the state's 2-acre/setback test with their own discretion, so the town chosen matters as much as the parcel.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures found for indoor dismantling-warehouse models (building SF per vehicle processed, required clear height, floor drains/oil-water separators). Unverified.
- No sourced guidance found on rail/port proximity or distance-to-shredder economics for MA/RI specifically (the major shredders/export docks at Everett MA and Providence's Allens Avenue are known to me from background knowledge only; unverified here).
- No MA statewide minimum acreage found; MA leaves "suitable premises" to the local board and zoning. Individual town bylaws were not surveyed.
- No sourced paving/drainage cost per acre found.

## 2. Industrial land and IOS market: Greater Boston, Route 128/495, Worcester, South Coast, Rhode Island

### Takeaway
Industrial buildings in Greater Boston are around 7.9% vacant with asking rents near $15/SF NNN (Q1 2026), but land suitable for outdoor storage is a separate, much tighter market: brokers describe infill IOS inside Boston as "nearly nonexistent," institutional capital is competing for it, and published per-acre lease comps are scarce. Closed-sale evidence found puts usable industrial/IOS land at roughly $190K to $770K per acre in MA (2025, small sample, derived) and about $300K per acre in Cranston RI (March 2025, derived).

### Cited Findings
**Greater Boston industrial fundamentals (buildings)**

- Q1 2026 asking rents by submarket: Urban $28.06/SF NNN; Metro North $19.46; Metro West $13.70; Metro South $13.56; Worcester Metro $10.64 (lowest, attributed to functional obsolescence of inventory). — [CBRE Q1 2026](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/89113539-836f-4ccf-a246-60df5bd7ebbd-1498387585/Q1_2026_Boston_Industrial_Figu.pdf)
- Q1 2026 availability by submarket: Urban 6.9%; Metro West 7.9%; Worcester Metro 9.5%; Metro North 9.9%; Metro South 12.2% (big-box speculative construction). Shallow-bay buildings (<50,000 SF) run 4.7 points below overall availability. — [CBRE Q1 2026](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/89113539-836f-4ccf-a246-60df5bd7ebbd-1498387585/Q1_2026_Boston_Industrial_Figu.pdf)
- CBRE expects occupiers to keep renewing "or be more interested in property or land sales, citing cost savings." — [CBRE Q1 2026](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/89113539-836f-4ccf-a246-60df5bd7ebbd-1498387585/Q1_2026_Boston_Industrial_Figu.pdf)

**IOS (industrial outdoor storage) as an asset class and its scarcity**

**Rhode Island**

### Inferences
- Rhode Island is materially cheaper than Greater Boston on buildings (general industrial $7-$8.50/SF vs Boston metro average $15.19 and Worcester $10.64), and the one RI land comp (~$300K/acre) sits below the MA IOS comps nearer Boston (~$690K-$770K/acre). The sample is tiny; treat as directional.
- Rough MA ladder from the comps: ~$700K+/acre for IOS-ready land in the 128/495 north and Worcester-east corridor (Tewksbury, Shrewsbury) vs. under $200K/acre in outlying Worcester County (Sterling). Land inside Route 128 is likely well above this range, but no comp was found.
- The Springfield asking rate ($7K-$15K/acre/month, i.e. $84K-$180K/acre/year) looks high relative to land values there and may reflect a small 1-acre premium or an aspirational ask; I would not use it as a Greater Boston benchmark. Skinner's "$6-per-foot" outlier equals ~$261K/acre/year (derived, 43,560 SF x $6), which he explicitly says is not a benchmark.
- Institutional IOS buyers want clean, entitled, paved sites leased to credit tenants (trucking, equipment, fleet). A salvage operator competes with them for the same scarce zoning but is a less attractive tenant, which strengthens the case for owning.

## 4. Buying an existing yard: deal structure, license transferability, zoning status, valuation

### Takeaway
BizBuySell's sold-business data puts junk/salvage yards at a median of about 3.85x earnings and ~1.0x revenue (2021-2025), with small yards near 2x SDE and $2M-revenue yards above 4x; real estate should be valued separately. In both states the license does not simply ride with the sale: MA Class 3 licenses are annual, issued by the local board after an abutter-noticed hearing for any "original" license, and RI requires DBR application and approval before any transfer.

### Cited Findings
**Valuation**
- BizBuySell junk/salvage yard benchmarks (sold listings 2021-2025): earnings multiple bottom quartile 2.25, average 3.27, median 3.85, top quartile 4.38; revenue multiple bottom quartile 0.56, average 0.86, median 1.03, top quartile 1.05 (search excerpt; page returned 403 on direct fetch). — [BizBuySell valuation benchmarks: junk/salvage yard](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/junk-salvage-yard/)
- Same source: a yard with about $2MM revenue would likely sell above 4x earnings, while one under $500K revenue would trade closer to 2x owner's discretionary earnings; typical sale prices run about $500K to $1.5MM, with $2MM+ not uncommon. — [BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/junk-salvage-yard/)
- General small-business valuation caution (auto-repair context, adjacent not direct): SDE suits owner-operated businesses, while institutional buyers apply multiples to normalized EBITDA; applying an EBITDA multiple to SDE overstates value because SDE includes the owner's pay. Auto repair SDE multiples are cited at 2x-3.5x. — [Clearly Acquired](https://www.clearlyacquired.com/blog/auto-repair-valuation-tools-sde-vs-ebitda-multiples); [AutoLeap](https://autoleap.com/blog/how-to-value-an-auto-repair-business/)

**Massachusetts Class 3 license (MGL c.140 s.59)**
- Licenses "shall expire on January first following the date of issue unless sooner revoked"; the fee is set locally and capped at $200. — [MGL c.140 s.59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- "No original license or permit shall be issued hereunder to a person within said class 3 until after a hearing, of which seven days' notice shall have been given to the owners of the property abutting on the premises." Outside Boston the board may waive the annual hearing for renewals. — [MGL c.140 s.59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- The board must be satisfied the applicant has a suitable place of business; permits for a change of location or additions to licensed premises are granted separately and attached to the license; licenses are revocable after hearing. The section as read does not contain an ownership-transfer mechanism. — [MGL c.140 s.59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59)
- Local practice: Boston requires a public hearing for new applicants and for all Class 3 renewals; Cambridge requires new applicants to appear before the Board of License Commissioners; Framingham's form lists "New Application" and "Transfer" as separate application types (search excerpts). — [Boston Police licensing](https://police.boston.gov/become-an-approved-motor-vehicle-dealer/); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridgema.gov/iwantto/applyforausedcardealerclassiiiiiilicenses); [Framingham form](https://www.framinghamma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/26238)

**Rhode Island license**
- "A business license cannot be transferred to another person without prior application to and approval by DBR." A licensed owner cannot lease the property to others to conduct salvage activity; the operator must own the licensed business, be its employee, or receive a DBR-approved transfer. — [RI DBR FAQ](https://dbr.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- RI requires owner/manager criminal background reports, a $10,000 surety bond, $750 per location per year, proof of local zoning compliance, and any local junk/second-hand license the town issues. — [RI DBR FAQ](https://dbr.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because the MA license is personal to the licensee, annual, and the statute provides no transfer mechanism, a buyer in an asset purchase should expect to file as a new ("original") Class 3 applicant with an abutter-noticed hearing, and should make closing contingent on issuance of the license and on written confirmation of zoning status. A listing's statement that licenses "can be conveyed" (Orange) should be read as "the seller will cooperate," not as a legal entitlement.
- A stock/membership-interest purchase keeps the licensed entity intact (and may avoid an "original" license hearing, subject to the local board's rules on change of control), but the buyer then inherits the entity's entire history, including operator liability for contamination, unpaid taxes, and past violations. For a contaminated-use business this usually argues for an asset purchase plus a new license, or a stock purchase only with heavy indemnity/insurance. This is my reasoning, not a sourced rule; confirm with counsel.
- In RI, the DBR rule that a licensee cannot lease the yard to another operator means "lease the seller's licensed yard and operate under his license" is not available; the tenant-operator needs its own license/transfer approval.
- Valuation practice implied by the benchmarks: value the operating business on SDE (2x-4x), value the real estate separately by appraisal net of environmental cost-to-cure, and count inventory once (not both inside the multiple and as an add-on).

### Gaps
- No MA statute or case was found stating in terms that Class 3 licenses are "not transferable"; the conclusion is drawn from the statute's structure (annual, personal, original-license hearing) and local forms. Whether a given town treats a change of ownership as "transfer" or "new" must be asked locally. Unverified.
- Grandfathered (pre-existing nonconforming) zoning status: no source retrieved. From background knowledge (unverified here): MGL c.40A s.6 protects lawful pre-existing nonconforming uses, local bylaws commonly treat a nonconforming use as abandoned after roughly two years of non-use, and expansion typically needs a special-permit finding. A buyer should get a zoning determination letter before closing.
- No salvage-specific EBITDA multiples from DealStats or Peak Business Valuation were retrieved; the "2-4x SDE" range rests on the BizBuySell data only.
- No sourced method for salvage inventory valuation (cost vs. percentage of list vs. scrap-floor value) was found.
- Seller-motivation evidence is anecdotal (family tenures in listings); no survey data found.

## 5. Contamination: contaminants, MA c.21E / MCP, RI statute, federal CERCLA

### Takeaway
In Massachusetts, cleanup duties attach to current owners and operators under c.21E and the MCP, are run by private Licensed Site Professionals, and carry notification clocks of 2 hours, 72 hours or 120 days from knowledge; a buyer who did not cause the contamination can earn "eligible person" liability protection, but only after reaching a permanent solution. Rhode Island and federal law both protect a "bona fide prospective purchaser," but only if the diligence and paperwork are done before closing and obligations are kept afterward.

### Cited Findings
**Typical contaminants / evidence from actual salvage sites**
- Stormwater pollutants identified at auto salvage yards include oil, heavy metals, acids and other compounds (EPA, older release; search excerpt). — [EPA newsroom archive](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/b2929b067af7231b852574d7006255cd.html)
- Northbridge, MA: MassDEP penalized D & G Recycling, Inc. $31,000 for violating waste-site cleanup regulations at a property used for auto repair and auto salvage since 1936, with three underground storage tanks (gasoline and waste oil) abandoned in place since 2005 (search excerpt). — [MassDEP press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/recycling-company-penalized-for-violating-waste-site-cleanup-regulations-at-their-northbridge-property)
- Springfield, MA: a draft Analysis of Brownfields Cleanup Alternatives for a former salvage facility lists scrap metal, glass, ceramics, plastic, car parts and empty metal drums on site, and treats former junkyard use itself as a recognized environmental condition (search excerpt; cost figures not seen). — [City of Springfield, NS Tapley Draft ABCA](https://www.springfield-ma.gov/planning/fileadmin/Planning_files/Economic_Development/NS_Tapley_-_Draft_ABCA.pdf)
- EPA Region 1 settled alleged Clean Water Act (stormwater) violations with a Massachusetts auto salvage company (search excerpt). — [EPA news release](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/massachusetts-auto-salvage-company-settles-alleged-clean-water-act-violations)

**Massachusetts: c.21E and the MCP (310 CMR 40.0000)**
- Cleanups are governed by c.21E and the Massachusetts Contingency Plan; parties conducting assessment and cleanup must hire a Licensed Site Professional (LSP), and MassDEP relies on LSPs to oversee most sites (search excerpts). — [MassDEP, Waste Site Cleanup program](https://www.mass.gov/guides/the-waste-site-cleanup-program); [MassDEP brownfields fact sheet](https://www.mass.gov/doc/fact-sheet-brownfields-and-waste-site-cleanup-programs/download)
- Notification tiers: conditions requiring notice within 2 hours of obtaining knowledge; within 72 hours; and within 120 days. The 2- and 72-hour notices are by telephone with written follow-up; 120-day notices are written; all use the Release Notification Form (BWSC103). Sites with 2- or 72-hour conditions must also perform an Immediate Response Action. — [MassDEP, Making Progress Cleaning Up Contaminated Properties](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/making-progress-cleaning-up-contaminated-properties); [MassDEP "Right from the Start"](https://www.mass.gov/doc/right-from-the-start-complying-with-mcp-notifications-and-preliminary-response-action/download)
- The LSP personally has no obligation under c.21E or the MCP to notify MassDEP; the duty sits with the owner/operator and other responsible persons. — [Bowditch real estate blog](https://www.bowditch.com/bowditchrealestateblog/2013/09/24/who-is-required-to-notify-massdep-of-a-release-of-oil-or-hazardous-material/?output=pdf)
- Missed MCP deadlines can trigger additional documentation, enforcement and penalties, or new/higher fees; MassDEP publishes a timelines-and-fees schedule (2018 document, fees effective 2014; current amounts not verified). — [MassDEP MCP timelines and fees](https://www.mass.gov/doc/mcp-timelines-and-fees-2018-0/download)
- Brownfields Act (c.206 of the Acts of 1998), "eligible person" protection (c.21E s.5C): once an eligible person achieves a permanent solution meeting MassDEP standards, liability is ended for Commonwealth claims for response action costs and for third-party claims for contribution, response costs and property damage under c.21E and common-law property damage. An eligible person is a current owner or operator who did not cause or contribute to the contamination (search excerpts). — [Mass.gov summary of the Brownfields Act](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/summary-of-the-brownfields-act-chapter-206-of-the-acts-of-1998); [MassDEP brownfields liability relief](https://www.mass.gov/doc/brownfields-liability-relief/download)
- An eligible person who starts cleanup and sells is protected once a later eligible person reaches a permanent solution or remedy operation status, provided the first complied with c.21E while owning/operating. — [Mass.gov Brownfields Act summary](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/summary-of-the-brownfields-act-chapter-206-of-the-acts-of-1998)
- Where statutory protection does not fit (for example only a temporary solution is achievable), the Attorney General's Brownfields Covenant Not to Sue program (940 CMR 23) can supply liability protection. — [940 CMR 23](https://www.mass.gov/doc/940-cmr-23-brownfields-covenant-not-to-sue-agreements/download)
- Brownfields Tax Credit: extended five years; response actions must commence on or before August 5, 2028 and net response and removal costs must be incurred before January 1, 2029; credit of up to 50% of eligible costs, 50% applying where the cleanup leaves no activity and use limitation (search excerpts; Verrill page returned 403). — [Mass.gov Brownfields Tax Credit](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-brownfields-tax-credit-btc); [Verrill](https://www.verrill-law.com/blog/massachusetts-extends-brownfields-tax-credit-program/); [MA tax expenditure budget FY25](https://budget.digital.mass.gov/govbudget/fy25/tax-expenditure-budget/personal-income-tax/credits-against-tax/1-608/)
- MassDevelopment Brownfields Redevelopment Fund: created 1998 to fund/finance assessment and remediation; a July 2025 round awarded $6,519,000 across 20 sites. — [Mass.gov press release](https://www.mass.gov/news/healey-driscoll-administration-announces-65-million-to-assess-clean-up-brownfields); [MassDevelopment Brownfields annual report 2025](https://www.massdevelopment.com/assets/document/annual-reports/BAR_2025.pdf)

**Rhode Island: Industrial Property Remediation and Reuse Act (RIGL 23-19.14)**
- A former owner/operator, another responsible party, or anyone who held more than a 10% equitable or legal interest in the site or the contamination-related operations cannot be a bona fide prospective purchaser (BFPP). — [RIGL 23-19.14-3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE23/23-19.14/23-19.14-3.htm)
- A BFPP is not a responsible party if it holds a remedial decision letter and is actively implementing the approved remedy, a letter of compliance, or an enforceable settlement agreement. — [RIGL 23-19.14-7](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE23/23-19.14/23-19.14-7.htm)
- As enacted in 1997, a BFPP is a purchaser who has documented in writing an intent to buy the contaminated property and has offered fair market value. — [RI Public Laws 1997 ch. 41](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/PublicLaws97/law97041.htm)
- RIDEM publishes a BFPP Certification Statement form in which the buyer represents that it meets the criteria. — [RIDEM form](https://dem.ri.gov/media/20221/download)
- A 2022 bill (H 7234) proposed that BFPP status, once certified to and acknowledged by RIDEM, last up to one year after purchase; enactment NOT confirmed. — [2022 H 7234](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText22/HouseText22/H7234.htm)
- RIDEM runs an Environmental Results Program for auto salvage yard facilities and maintains a salvage yard list; the DBR license application asks whether the applicant completed it. — [RIDEM yard list](https://dem.ri.gov/programs/benviron/assist/asy/pdf/yardlist.pdf); [RI DBR Auto Wrecking Application](https://dbr.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur696/files/2022-04/Auto_Wrecking_Application.pdf)

**Federal CERCLA**
- CERCLA s.107(r) protects a bona fide prospective purchaser from Superfund owner/operator liability if it acquires property after January 11, 2002. — [EPA, Bona Fide Prospective Purchasers](https://epa.gov/enforcement/bona-fide-prospective-purchasers)
- The buyer must conduct "all appropriate inquiries" prior to or on the date of acquisition. — [Federal Register, AAI final rule (2005)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2005/11/01/05-21455/standards-and-practices-for-all-appropriate-inquiries)
- BFPP status must be maintained through continuing obligations after acquisition, including taking reasonable steps to stop continuing releases. — [EPA Common Elements guidance (2019)](https://epa.gov/sites/default/files/2019-08/documents/common-elements-guide-mem-2019.pdf)
- EPA brownfield cleanup grants have been listed at $200,000 per site with a 20% cost share (older fact sheet; current amounts not verified, and these grants generally go to public/nonprofit applicants). — [EPA MA brownfields fact sheet (2014)](https://archive.epa.gov/region1/brownfields/web/pdf/2014mafactsheet.pdf)

### Inferences
- The MA protection is back-loaded: an innocent buyer is still a current "owner/operator" on day one and only gets s.5C relief after the LSP files a permanent solution. Buying a dirty yard therefore means committing to finish the cleanup, not just to having been innocent.
- Operating a salvage yard after purchase creates a commingling problem: new drips on old contamination make it hard to prove the buyer "did not cause or contribute," which threatens eligible-person status in MA and BFPP "continuing obligations" federally. A documented baseline (Phase II at closing) plus impervious surfaces and fluid-management practices is the practical defense.
- A knowledge-triggered clock (2 hr / 72 hr / 120 days) means Phase II sampling can itself create a reporting obligation for whoever owns the site when the data come back; purchase agreements should say who reports and who pays if testing finds a reportable condition before closing.
- Since the 50% Brownfields Tax Credit rate applies only where no AUL is left, a cleanup that ends in an AUL (the cheaper and more common endpoint for an industrial yard) earns a lower rate (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Not sourced here (background knowledge, unverified): c.21E s.5 liability is strict and joint-and-several for current owners/operators and past owners/operators at the time of release; typical salvage-yard contaminants beyond the cited oil/metals/acids include lead (batteries, wheel weights), mercury switches, PCBs (older fluids/shredder residue), chlorinated solvents (parts washing), antifreeze/glycols, refrigerants, and asbestos (brake/clutch). No source was found tying PFAS specifically to salvage yards.
- Brownfields Tax Credit: the commonly cited 25% rate where an AUL is used, and the requirements that the taxpayer be an eligible person and the site be in an Economically Distressed Area, were NOT confirmed in retrieved sources. Whether a given town (for example Waltham vs. Worcester, Fall River, New Bedford) qualifies must be checked on mass.gov.
- MassDevelopment Brownfields Redevelopment Fund caps (often cited as up to $100,000 for assessment and $500,000 for remediation) were NOT confirmed.
- Activity and Use Limitation (AUL) mechanics (recorded deed notice restricting uses/requiring maintenance of caps) were not retrieved from a primary source.
- Current MCP fees (Tier classification annual compliance fees, permanent solution fee) not verified; only a 2018 schedule was located.
- RIDEM Remediation Regulations (250-RICR-140-30-1) details (notification triggers, Environmental Land Usage Restriction/ELUR, Letter of Compliance process) not retrieved.
- Tenant/operator and lender exemptions under the MA Brownfields Act were not confirmed (mass.gov returned 403).

## 6. Due diligence: Phase I / Phase II, cleanup costs, insurance, deal structures, lease vs. buy

### Takeaway
A Phase I ESA costs a few thousand dollars and two to four weeks and is the ticket to federal liability defenses; for a salvage yard a Phase II (tens of thousands of dollars, 4-8+ weeks) should be assumed because the use itself is a recognized environmental condition. No reliable published cleanup-cost range for MA/RI salvage yards was found, and pollution insurance generally excludes whatever the Phase II finds.

### Cited Findings
- ASTM E1527-21 took effect February 13, 2023 as the EPA-recognized standard for All Appropriate Inquiries; the older E1527-13 stopped qualifying on February 13, 2024. — [American Bar Association, Probate & Property (Nov/Dec 2023)](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/real_property_trust_estate/publications/probate-property-magazine/2023/november-december/environmental-diligence-the-era-pfas-pitfalls-simply-ordering-phase-i/)
- Phase I cost (2025-2026 consultant quotes, not official benchmarks): $2,800-$8,500 for typical commercial sites per one consultant; $2,200-$4,000 per another; overall range seen $1,800-$10,000. — [Terrapin CG](https://terrapincg.com/news/environmental-site-assessment-commercial-construction-2026); [Farmonaut](https://farmonaut.com/?p=173728)
- Phase I timing: 10-15 business days per one consultant; 2-4 weeks per another. — [Fehr Graham](https://www.fehrgraham.com/about-us/blog/phase-i-environmental-site-assessment-cost-fg); [DealRoom](https://dealroom.net/blog/environmental-due-diligence)
- Phase II cost: "$5,000 or more"; $10,000-$15,000+; $12,000-$120,000 with a $48,000 case study. Timing 4-8 weeks. — [Transect](https://www.transect.com/blog/new-phase-1-esa); [DealRoom](https://dealroom.net/blog/environmental-due-diligence); [Terrapin CG](https://terrapincg.com/news/environmental-site-assessment-commercial-construction-2026)
- A Phase II is triggered when the Phase I identifies a Recognized Environmental Condition (REC); the Springfield ABCA treats former junkyard use as a REC. — [Daily Journal](https://www.dailyjournal.com/articles/387723-why-cities-can-t-skip-phase-i-environmental-due-diligence); [Springfield draft ABCA](https://www.springfield-ma.gov/planning/fileadmin/Planning_files/Economic_Development/NS_Tapley_-_Draft_ABCA.pdf)
- Shelf life: a Phase I is good for about one year with some components needing update after six months; under AAI the report generally must be within 180 days of acquisition (secondary sources; verify against 40 CFR Part 312). — [City of Longmont definitions](https://longmontcolorado.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Definitions-of-Assessments-Surveys-Plans.pdf); [Terrapin CG](https://terrapincg.com/news/environmental-site-assessment-commercial-construction-2026)
- Pollution Legal Liability (PLL) insurance generally covers pre-existing contamination that was unknown to the insured at binding; known conditions are typically excluded, though some may be covered by separate cost-cap products or written excess of a contractual indemnity, and insurers are increasingly unwilling to underwrite known conditions broadly. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/researching-the-right-insurance-policy-can-save-your-m-a-deal); [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/pollution-protection-improves-environment-for-real-estate-deals/); [Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/gradual-pollution-risks-may-be-insurable/)
- PLL terms for transactions are usually multi-year, with 3, 5, 7 and 10-year policies common for "clean" sites. — [GlobeSt Q&A (2017)](https://www.globest.com/amp/2017/08/25/qa-part-two-using-environmental-insurance-to-close-real-estate-transactions/)
- Dated premium indications only: premiums of 2%-6% of estimated cleanup cost (cost-cap context); in 2001, known-problem sites started around $50,000. Not current pricing. — [Insurance Journal (2001)](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2001/07/23/18589.htm)
- Counsel, not only the broker, should review whether policy wording actually covers the identified risks. — [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/publications/eliminate-environmental-roadblocks-through-insurance.pdf)
- RI: a property owner holding a salvage license cannot lease the property for others to run salvage activity under that license. — [RI DBR FAQ](https://dbr.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)
- Brokers report that IOS lease comps are scarce partly because many users own their sites, and that towns resist IOS permits/rezoning. — [NEREJ, Skinner](https://www.nerej.com/what-is-industrial-outdoor-storage-land-really-worth-anyway-by-david-skinner); [NEREJ, Wagner](https://nerej.com/new-england-ios-user-demand-is-poised-to-increase-and-vacancy-to-decrease-in-2025-by-devon-wagner)

### Inferences
- Budget on the order of $3K-$8K for the Phase I and $25K-$75K+ for a multi-acre salvage-yard Phase II (soil borings, monitoring wells, sediment/stormwater outfalls), with 2-3 months before results are usable; this is my extrapolation from general commercial ranges, since no salvage-specific quote was found.
- Sequence for a buyer: (1) Phase I by an environmental professional meeting E1527-21, timed inside 180 days of closing; (2) Phase II negotiated in the purchase agreement, including who must report findings to MassDEP/RIDEM; (3) LSP (MA) cost-to-closure estimate; (4) price reduction, seller-funded escrow/holdback sized to the LSP estimate, and seller indemnity (worth only as much as the seller's remaining assets); (5) PLL for unknowns; (6) in RI, BFPP certification to RIDEM and a remedial decision letter/settlement before closing.
- PLL is a complement, not a substitute: for a salvage yard the known conditions are usually the expensive ones and will be excluded, so the escrow/price adjustment has to carry those.
- Lease vs. buy: leasing avoids owner liability but an operator-tenant is still an "operator" under c.21E, landlords and institutional IOS owners have little reason to accept a salvage tenant when cleaner users compete for the same scarce land, and in RI the license cannot be borrowed from the landlord. A tenant should insist on a pre-lease baseline assessment so later contamination is not attributed to it; a landlord will insist on an exit assessment, indemnity and probably pollution insurance. No source quantifying landlord reluctance was found; this is reasoning from the IOS market commentary.

### Gaps
- No reliable published cleanup-cost range for MA/RI auto salvage yards was found. The Springfield ABCA likely contains alternative-by-alternative cost estimates but the figures were not retrieved. Unverified.
- No current (2025-2026) PLL premium quotes for a salvage/IOS site; the only numbers found are from 2001-2017.
- No sourced examples of escrow/holdback sizing in salvage-yard deals.
- 180-day AAI component windows should be confirmed in 40 CFR 312.20.
- No source on lender requirements (SBA 7(a)/504 environmental policy requires Phase I/II for high-risk NAICS uses, from background knowledge; unverified here).

## 7. Property taxes, wetlands and stormwater improvements

### Takeaway
Little was retrieved on these points; they are largely gaps. What the evidence does show is that stormwater is an active enforcement area for salvage yards and that leading operators pave their yards, which makes impervious surface, drainage and wetlands buffers real capital items in any acquisition budget.

### Cited Findings
- EPA has pursued and settled Clean Water Act stormwater cases against a Massachusetts auto salvage company; EPA identifies oil, heavy metals and acids as salvage-yard stormwater pollutants (search excerpts). — [EPA news release](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/massachusetts-auto-salvage-company-settles-alleged-clean-water-act-violations); [EPA archive](https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/b2929b067af7231b852574d7006255cd.html)
- A Florida self-service operator paved its entire yard in concrete; a Worcester-area yard for sale advertises about 13 acres of concrete surface as a selling feature (search excerpts). — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/article/garden-street-u-pull-it-florida-profile); [Crexi 267 Granite St](https://www.crexi.com/properties/249309/massachusetts-267-granite-st)
- Listings show large shares of yard parcels are unused: Berlin MA uses about 7.5 of roughly 25 acres, with 17.5 acres undeveloped. — [LoopNet Berlin](https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/76-Coburn-Rd-Berlin-MA/39054516/)
- RIDEM operates an Environmental Results Program specific to auto salvage yards, and the RI license application asks about it. — [RI DBR Auto Wrecking Application](https://dbr.ri.gov/sites/g/files/xkgbur696/files/2022-04/Auto_Wrecking_Application.pdf)
- RI DBR's license review covers payment of taxes as well as RIDEM compliance. — [RI DBR FAQ](https://dbr.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gross acreage overstates usable yard: undeveloped back acreage on old yards is often wetlands, buffer or slope. Per-acre price comparisons should use usable, permitted acres.
- Expanding the working area or paving an existing yard is likely to trigger local conservation commission review and stormwater design standards, so "we will pave it later" should be priced and permitted before closing rather than assumed.
- Under a typical NNN industrial/IOS lease the tenant pays the property taxes, and capital improvements such as paving and drainage structures stay with the landlord's land at lease end, which is another reason salvage users tend to own.

### Gaps
- Property taxes: no commercial/industrial tax rates were retrieved. MA municipalities may adopt a split rate with a higher commercial/industrial/personal property (CIP) rate; rates by town are published annually by the MA Division of Local Services and should be pulled for candidate towns (for example Waltham, Worcester, Fall River, New Bedford) and for RI municipalities (Providence, Cranston, Johnston, North Smithfield). Unverified.
- No sourced detail retrieved on the MA Wetlands Protection Act (MGL c.131 s.40; 100-ft buffer zone, 200-ft Riverfront Area) or the RI Freshwater Wetlands rules as applied to salvage yards.
- No sourced detail on the EPA Multi-Sector General Permit (Sector M, automobile salvage yards; EPA is the NPDES permitting authority in Massachusetts, RIDEM in Rhode Island), SWPPP preparation costs, or benchmark monitoring. These are from background knowledge and unverified here.
- No sourced cost per acre for paving, oil/water separators, or stormwater retrofit.
- No sourced examples of how MA/RI landlords and salvage tenants allocate stormwater/wetlands capital costs in leases.
