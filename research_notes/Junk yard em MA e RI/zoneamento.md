# Zoning and land-use rules for junk yards / auto salvage / auto dismantling in Massachusetts and Rhode Island (as of Nov. 2026)

Researcher's note on reliability: ecode360.com, codelibrary.amlegal.com, justia.com and the Providence ordinance PDFs returned 403/404 in this session, so most municipal use tables could NOT be read directly. Items are tagged **[VERIFIED]** (I read the primary text), **[SNIPPET]** (seen only in a search-result summary, not the full page) or listed under Gaps. Worcester (full ordinance PDF) and Waltham (zoning text via Zoneomics mirror, not the use table itself) are the two municipal codes I actually read. Nothing below is legal advice; every site needs a zoning determination from the local building commissioner.

## 1. How MA zoning (MGL c.40A) treats junk yards: districts, special permit vs. by-right vs. prohibited; concrete municipal examples

### Takeaway
In the Massachusetts codes I could read, a junk yard/auto salvage yard is never a by-right use: it is confined to industrial/manufacturing districts and requires a discretionary special permit (Waltham: City Council special permit; Worcester: special permit in ML/MG districts only), on top of the separate Class 3 license. For Waltham specifically, the code does contemplate junkyards and "auto recycling centers" as special-permit uses, but I could not read the Table of Uses to confirm which districts (if any) carry an "S" rather than "N".

### Cited Findings
**State framework**
- [VERIFIED] A special permit needs a supermajority: two-thirds vote of boards with more than five members, four of five on a five-member board, unanimous on a three-member board (c.40A §9) — [MGL c.40A §9](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/Chapter40A/Section9) (text seen via search summary of the statute page).
- [SNIPPET] Special permit denials are appealed under c.40A §17 (Land Court/Superior Court); courts uphold denials unless based on a legally untenable ground or arbitrary/capricious — [Sunpin v. ZBA of Petersham, mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/doc/sunpin-energy-services-llc-v-zoning-board-of-appeals-of-petersham-24p0018/download).

**Waltham** (reader's home city)
- [VERIFIED via Zoneomics mirror of Waltham Zoning Code ch. 3] Junkyard is a defined use (Sec. 3.257): "Any establishment or place of business which is maintained, operated or used for the keeping or selling of junk"; garbage dumps and sanitary landfills are excluded from the term — [Waltham zoning ch. 3, Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [VERIFIED, same source] "Automobile recycling center: See Junkyard, Section 3.257" (Sec. 3.259), i.e., Waltham treats auto recycling the same as a junkyard — [Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [VERIFIED, same source] Sec. 3.633 (Junkyard): "Allowed by special permit of the City Council after a landscaping and buffering site plan..."; Sec. 3.631: "The operation of an auto recycling center shall be allowed by special permit of the City Council" — [Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [VERIFIED, same source] Related uses also need City Council special permits: sale of secondhand motor vehicles (Sec. 3.632), and autobody shops (Sec. 3.626: "no outdoor autobody work shall be permitted; the outdoor storage of vehicles shall be prohibited") — [Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [VERIFIED, same source] Waltham nonconforming rules: "No nonconforming use which shall have been abandoned for more than two years shall be resumed" (Sec. 3.7213); enlargement up to 10% of ground floor area by Board of Appeals special permit (Sec. 3.7222), larger expansion by City Council special permit; change to "a similar use of not less restricted character" by Board of Appeals special permit (Sec. 3.7223); a nonconforming use changed to conforming cannot revert (Sec. 3.7211) — [Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [SNIPPET] The official Waltham code is on eCode360 (code WA1697); the Table of Uses is Sec. 3.4; Waltham general ordinances also have a "junk motor vehicles" provision at §8-53 — [eCode360 Waltham index](https://www.ecode360.com/WA1697/index?letter=T).

**Worcester** (ordinance "as amended through January 13, 2026", read in full)
- [VERIFIED] "OPEN LOT STORAGE – A location providing storage, handling or bailing of second hand lumber or other used building materials, metals, junk, scrap, paper, rags, unrepaired or uncleaned containers, or other articles of salvage; wrecking and/or dismantling of motor vehicles." — [Worcester Zoning Ordinance PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).
- [VERIFIED] Table 4.1, Manufacturing Use row 8 "Open lot storage, underground storage, salvage recycling operations, refuse transfer station facility": **N** in every residential (RS, RL, RG), business (BO, BL, BG), IP and Institutional district; **SP** (special permit) in ML-0.5, ML-1, ML-2, MG-0.5, MG-1, MG-2 and A-1 (airport). Nowhere "Y" — [Worcester Zoning Ordinance PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).
- [VERIFIED] By contrast, auto/truck body or paint shops are **Y** (by right) in all ML and MG districts, and "storage of materials and equipment not [in] enclosed buildings" is Y in ML/MG — [same PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).
- [VERIFIED] Note 6 to Table 4.1: where open-lot storage of junk/scrap or "wrecking or dismantling of motor vehicles" is allowed, "all dust and dirt incident to storage or handling shall be effectively confined to the premises." Note 3: where garages/body shops are allowed, all repairs must be inside a building and "All outside storage of wrecked vehicles or parts shall be screened from the street and neighborhood." Note 5: materials stored above 4 feet in unenclosed premises must be surrounded by a visual barrier — [same PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).

**Boston**
- [SNIPPET] Boston's Article 8 use table marks each use allowed, conditional (needs a conditional use permit from the Zoning Board of Appeal) or forbidden; it was rewritten by Text Amendment 470 effective April 23, 2024; forbidden uses can exist only as lawful nonconforming uses — [Boston code Art. 8, Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/boston-MA/chapter_13).
- [SNIPPET] The Boston code has distinct legacy use items for "wrecking or dismantling of motor vehicles" (item 57), "outdoor storage of damaged motor vehicles" (57A) and repair garage (60) — [Boston code, American Legal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/boston/latest/boston_ma/0-0-0-12225) (page returned 403; wording from search summary only).
- [VERIFIED] In Boston the annual hearing for Class 3 license renewal cannot be waived (unlike other municipalities) — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59).

**Smaller towns**
- [SNIPPET] Salem's zoning table lists "Junkyard or automobile graveyard" as an enumerated use (district status not seen) — [Salem ordinance file](https://salemma.gov/legal-department-and-records-access-officer/files/marijuana-ordinance).
- [VERIFIED via news] Ashfield adopted a bylaw banning junkyards in 1995; a yard at 995 West Road survives only on a claimed pre-1995 nonconforming status and is being litigated before the ZBA in 2026 — [Greenfield Recorder, Apr. 24 2026](https://recorder.com/2026/04/24/zoning-board-ashfield-junkyard/).
- [SNIPPET] One MA town code allows only a one-year license for no more than two unregistered/wrecked vehicles on a premises, with neighborhood-protection conditions — [eCode360](https://ecode360.com/34392303) (town not identified in snippet).
- [VERIFIED] Freetown: the building inspector determined Excel Recycling's auto-shredding facility (5-acre site, up to 60 tons/hour) needed a special permit; the ZBA rejected a residents' petition to stop it (2016) — [Waste Dive, Sept. 16 2016](https://www.wastedive.com/news/excel-recycling-gets-green-light-for-construction-of-scrap-facility-in-mass/426404/).

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern (Waltham, Worcester, Ashfield, Freetown) is: prohibited in residential/commercial zones; discretionary special permit in industrial zones; outright bans in some small towns. A new yard therefore needs a supermajority vote of a political or quasi-political body (in Waltham, the City Council), which is where neighborhood opposition is most effective.
- For a Waltham used-car dealer, note the dealership itself (secondhand motor vehicle sales) already sits under a City Council special permit; adding dismantling would be a different, more intensive use category (junkyard/auto recycling center) requiring a new special permit plus a "landscaping and buffering site plan", not an accessory extension of the Class 2 dealership.
- Worcester shows the practical gap between "auto body/repair" (by right in industrial zones) and "wrecking/dismantling" (special permit): classification of the activity by the building commissioner is the first decisive step.

### Gaps

Must be checked at ecode360.com/WA1697 or with the Waltham Building Department. Whether any currently licensed Class 3 yard exists in Waltham was not researched.
- Boston (current Art. 8 table entry for vehicle wrecking/junk), Brockton, Lowell, Lawrence, Framingham, New Bedford, Fall River: use-table entries NOT obtained. No claims should be made for these cities without checking their ordinances.

Variances: under c.40A §10 use variances are allowed only if the local ordinance expressly authorizes them — I did not fetch §10 or confirm which of these cities permit use variances (unverified, from background knowledge).

## 2. How RI zoning (RIGL 45-24) treats junk yards; municipal examples

### Takeaway
Rhode Island layers a state auto-wrecking/salvage license (Dept. of Business Regulation) and state siting conditions (RIGL 5-21-4; Junkyard Control Act 24-14) on top of municipal zoning, where uses not listed in the use table are typically prohibited and listed heavy uses need a special use permit from the Zoning Board of Review. I could not read the use tables of Providence, Cranston, Pawtucket, Johnston, Warwick, Woonsocket or Central Falls, so district-level answers for RI remain unverified.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] RIGL §5-21-4 conditions for licensing an automobile junkyard (alternatives): (1) operated and maintained entirely within a building; or (2) operated "exclusively for the purpose of salvaging the value as scrap of the material collected" (not resale of parts) and located in a built-up industrial area, or contiguous to a railroad siding, or on/contiguous to docking facilities; or (3) more than 1,000 ft from the nearest edge of any interstate/primary highway, more than 600 ft from any other state highway, and "More than three hundred feet (300') from any park, bathing beach, playground, school, church, or cemetery" and not within ordinary view of them; or (4) screened from view by natural objects or "well-constructed and properly maintained fences at least six feet (6') high" acceptable to the city/town and specified on the license — [RIGL §5-21-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE5/5-21/5-21-4.htm).
- [SNIPPET] RI Junkyard Control Act (§24-14-4) bars junkyards within 1,000 ft of interstate/primary highway rights-of-way, with exceptions (e.g., not visible from the main traveled way; older versions excepted industrially zoned areas) — [RIGL §24-14-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE24/24-14/24-14-4.htm).
- [SNIPPET] The RI Department of Business Regulation issues state auto wrecking and salvage yard licenses; its FAQ notes that cities and towns issue their own local second-hand/junk licenses — [DBR Auto Wrecking & Salvage FAQs](https://dbr.ri.gov/documents/divisions/commlicensing/autowrecking/AutoWreckingSalvageFAQs.pdf); governing rules at [230/290-RICR 30-00-3](https://rules.sos.ri.gov/Regulations/Part/290-30-00-3).
- [SNIPPET] Providence: use matrix is Table 12-1 (Article 12); "Uses that are not included in the matrix are prohibited in all zones"; special use permits are decided by the Zoning Board of Review; in 2020 the City Plan Commission recommended denying an amendment that would have permitted a waste-facility use in W-3 and M-2 — [Providence CPC Referral 3472](https://www.providenceri.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Referral3472-amendment-waste_facilitypetition_and_recommendation.pdf).
- [SNIPPET] Cranston: Table of Principal Uses uses "Y = use allowed, S = use allowed by special permit, N = use not allowed" with M-1 and M-2 industrial columns; the table was amended as recently as January 20, 2026 — [Cranston code, Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/cranston-RI).
- [VERIFIED via Zoneomics] Cranston §17.32.010 standards for used car sales / auto-truck rental lots: minimum lot 12,000 sq ft, 100 ft frontage, setbacks 40 ft front / 20 ft rear / 20 ft side, and a solid wall, fence or tight evergreen hedge at least 5 ft high along rear/side lines adjoining residential districts — [Cranston ch. 17.32, Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/cranston-RI/chapter_8).
- [SNIPPET] Johnston has a specific ordinance on licensed automobile junkyards and salvage yards aimed at health/safety hazards (fire prevention and storage rules) — [Johnston code, eCode360](https://ecode360.com/14475814).
- [SNIPPET] Woonsocket: the city zoning officer recommended against renewing a junkyard license because three trailers and a mobile home filled with salvaged parts sat within a 15-foot buffer zone from abutting property lines; another yard owner (Don Senra, A&M Auto Parts) had his license renewed by the City Council "after months of debate" and sued the city (article undated in results; appears old) — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/ri-officials-support-license-for-salvage-yard).
- [SNIPPET] Foster: a junkyard operator who violated municipal license conditions and ignored a cease-and-desist received a second cease-and-desist in Sept. 2021 with a $100/day fine; the zoning board of review denied the appeal — [RI Supreme Court zoning summaries, Justia](https://zoningplanninglanduseopinions.justia.com/category/rhode-island-supreme-court/).

### Inferences
- RI state law (§5-21-4) effectively pre-screens sites: an open-air parts-resale yard must either meet the 1,000/600/300-ft distances or be fully screened by a 6-ft fence accepted by the municipality; an indoor operation or an industrial-area scrap-only operation qualifies by a separate route.
- Because Providence prohibits anything not in its use matrix, the classification question (is the business "vehicle repair", "outdoor storage yard", "recycling/materials processing" or an unlisted junkyard?) is decisive there.

## 3. Pre-existing nonconforming ("grandfathered") use: MA c.40A §6 and RI equivalent; why existing yards are valuable

### Takeaway
In both states a lawfully established yard may continue after zoning turns against it, and the right attaches to the land, not the owner, so it passes to a buyer; but it is fragile: MA lets towns extinguish it after two years of abandonment or non-use, RI presumes abandonment after one year of halted use, and any expansion in MA requires a "not substantially more detrimental" finding. This scarcity plus transferability is what makes an existing licensed, lawfully nonconforming yard far more valuable than raw industrial land.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] c.40A §6: extension or alteration of a pre-existing nonconforming use is not permitted "unless there is a finding by the permit granting authority" that the change "shall not be substantially more detrimental than the existing nonconforming use to the neighborhood" — [MGL c.40A §6](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter40A/Section6).
- [VERIFIED] c.40A §6: a local ordinance or by-law "may define and regulate nonconforming uses and structures abandoned or not used for a period of two years or more" (optional for the municipality) — [MGL c.40A §6](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter40A/Section6).
- [SNIPPET] Derby Refining Co. v. Chelsea, 407 Mass. 703 (1990): abandonment requires "the concurrence of two factors, (1) the intent to abandon and (2) voluntary conduct, whether affirmative or negative, which carries the implication of abandonment"; the protected nonconforming use is not confined to the existing user but runs with the land — [Derby Refining v. Chelsea, Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/massachusetts/supreme-court/1990/407-mass-703-3.html) (page 403; holding from search summary).
- [SNIPPET] Ka-Hur Enterprises v. ZBA of Provincetown (1996): where the bylaw so provides, simple cessation of a nonconforming use for two years or more results in loss of the protected use, independent of intent; MA courts recognise two separate routes to extinguishment: (a) abandonment and (b) non-use for two or more years — [NatLawReview summary](https://natlawreview.com/article/massachusetts-appeals-court-reaffirms-local-control-over-nonconforming-uses).
- [VERIFIED] Waltham: nonconforming use abandoned more than two years cannot be resumed; enlargement beyond 10% of ground-floor area needs a City Council special permit — [Waltham zoning ch. 3, Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [VERIFIED] Worcester Art. XVI §4(E): "If a nonconforming use or structure is abandoned, it may not be rehabilitated thereafter... a use or structure shall be presumed abandoned if over a period of two (2) years the use and/or use of the structure has discontinued"; a use destroyed by disaster may be restored if completed within two years and no larger; a nonconforming use changed to a more restricted use cannot change back. Worcester defines abandonment to include "the removal of characteristic equipment or furnishings, or the replacement of the nonconforming use with a conforming use" — [Worcester Zoning Ordinance PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).
- [VERIFIED] RI §45-24-39: "The zoning ordinance shall permit the continuation of nonconforming development"; "A zoning ordinance may provide that, if a nonconforming use is abandoned, it may not be reestablished"; abandonment requires an overt act or failure to act showing the owner no longer claims an interest; involuntary interruption (fire, natural catastrophe) does not establish intent; "if any nonconforming use is halted for a period of one year, the owner of the nonconforming use is presumed to have abandoned the nonconforming use, unless that presumption is rebutted by the presentation of sufficient evidence of intent not to abandon the use" — [RIGL §45-24-39](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE45/45-24/45-24-39.htm).
- [VERIFIED via news] Ashfield 2026: opponents argue that a yard that operated without the required license/permit was never "lawful" and so cannot be grandfathered; the building commissioner ruled (Nov. 2025) it pre-dated the 1995 ban and may continue but must be screened; ownership had been transferred within the family — [Greenfield Recorder](https://recorder.com/2026/04/24/zoning-board-ashfield-junkyard/).

### Inferences
- Due diligence when buying an existing yard should establish: (1) the use was lawful when begun (continuous Class 3 license history is the best evidence — the Ashfield dispute turns on exactly this); (2) no gap of two years (MA) / one year (RI) in actual operation; (3) the current footprint, vehicle count and activities do not exceed what existed when the use became nonconforming, since unpermitted expansion is not protected; (4) the buyer's intended changes (adding shredding/crushing, more acreage, a building) may each need a §6 finding or special permit.
- A seller who has let the Class 3 license lapse or stopped operating is selling a wasting asset: the clock toward loss of grandfathered status may already be running.
- RI's one-year presumption is shorter but rebuttable; MA's two-year non-use rule (where adopted locally) can operate regardless of intent.

### Gaps
- No junkyard-specific MA appellate holding on expansion of a nonconforming yard was retrieved (Town of Fairhaven v. Ben Prince & Sons, Mass. App. Ct. 1974, concerns whether land was "a storage place [for junk] or a junkyard" — holding not read: [link](https://hallapproved.com/ma/cases/appeals/1974/6581063/)).
- The standard MA three-part test for change in nonconforming use (Powers v. Building Inspector of Barnstable; Bridgewater v. Chuckran) was not fetched — unverified here.
- No data found on price premiums for grandfathered yards.

## 4. Dimensional/performance requirements; wetlands, floodplain and aquifer/groundwater overlays

### Takeaway
State licensing statutes in both MA and RI impose nearly identical siting conditions (indoor operation, or industrial-area scrap-only, or 1,000/600/300-ft separations, or screening by 6-ft fences), and MassDEP's drinking-water regulations require municipalities to prohibit "automobile graveyards and junkyards" in Zone II wellhead areas, which removes much otherwise industrial land from consideration. Local codes add screening, dust-confinement and buffer rules.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] MGL c.140 §59A: no Class 3 motor vehicle junk yard license unless the yard (a) is operated entirely within a building; or (b) salvages only scrap value (not resale parts) and is in a built-up industrial or commercial area, or beside a railroad siding, or on/contiguous to docking facilities; or (c) is more than "one thousand feet from the nearest edge of any highway on the interstate or primary system", more than 600 ft from any other state highway, and more than 300 ft from a park, bathing beach, playground, school, church or cemetery and not in "ordinary view therefrom"; or (d) is screened from view by natural objects or well-maintained fences at least six feet high acceptable to the city/town and meeting highway department regulations, specified on the license (application copy must go to the highway department within three working days) — [MGL c.140 §59A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59A).
- [VERIFIED] 310 CMR 22.21(2)(a)3: municipal wellhead protection controls must prohibit within Zone II (or Zone III in some cases) "automobile graveyards and junkyard, as defined in M.G.L. c. 140B, § 1", alongside landfills, bulk petroleum terminals and hazardous-waste facilities — [310 CMR 22.21, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/310-CMR-22-21).
- [SNIPPET] MassDEP publishes a Model Groundwater Protection District Bylaw (developed 1991) implementing 310 CMR 22.21(2), stricter than the regulation in places — [MassDEP model bylaw](https://www.mass.gov/doc/model-groundwater-protection-district-bylawordinance/download).
- [VERIFIED] Worcester Water Resources Protection Overlay District Table 12.1: "Sanitary landfill, junkyard, salvage yard/or other solid waste disposal" is **N** in GP-2 and **SP** in GP-3 — [Worcester Zoning Ordinance PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).
- [SNIPPET] Dover (MA) wellhead/groundwater bylaw and Harwich groundwater regulation list junkyards among prohibited uses — [Dover code](https://ecode360.com/10427069); [Harwich regulation](https://www.harwich-ma.gov/sites/g/files/vyhlif12326/f/pages/groundwater_protection_regulation.pdf).
- [VERIFIED] Worcester performance notes: dust and dirt confined to the premises; outside storage of wrecked vehicles or parts screened from street and neighborhood; visual barrier required for storage above 4 ft — [Worcester Zoning Ordinance PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).
- [VERIFIED] Waltham requires a "landscaping and buffering site plan" as part of the junkyard special permit — [Waltham zoning ch. 3, Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [SNIPPET] Woonsocket enforces a 15-foot buffer from abutting property lines for junkyards (storage inside the buffer was grounds to oppose renewal) — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/ri-officials-support-license-for-salvage-yard).
- [VERIFIED] RI §5-21-4 equivalents: 1,000 ft / 600 ft / 300 ft separations; fences at least 6 ft high — [RIGL §5-21-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE5/5-21/5-21-4.htm).

### Inferences
- In practice the screening alternative (6-ft fence/natural objects) is the route most urban yards rely on, since the 1,000/600/300-ft separations are hard to meet inside Route 128; a site visible from an interstate/primary highway faces the additional MassDOT/highway review built into §59A.
- Any candidate parcel should be checked against the municipal groundwater/aquifer overlay map and MassDEP Zone II maps before anything else: in Zone II the use is prohibited by state-mandated local control, and a special permit cannot cure that.

### Gaps
- No verified local numbers for minimum lot size, stacking height limits or hours of operation for junkyards in the listed cities (only Cranston's used-car-lot standards and Worcester's 4-ft visual barrier trigger were found).
- MA Wetlands Protection Act (c.131 §40; 100-ft buffer zone, 200-ft riverfront area, Conservation Commission Order of Conditions) and floodplain overlay interaction: not researched from primary sources in this session — unverified background knowledge only; no junkyard-specific wetlands enforcement case found.
- EPA/MassDEP stormwater (Multi-Sector General Permit, Sector M auto salvage) requirements not researched here (likely covered by another researcher).

## 5. Class 3 license hearing (MGL c.140 §59) vs. zoning; community opposition; recent cases

### Takeaway
The Class 3 license and zoning are separate, cumulative approvals: the licensing authority must hold a public hearing with seven days' notice to abutters before issuing an original Class 3 license, the license expires every January 1, and municipalities may regulate the location of Class 3 premises; zoning relief must be obtained independently. Both forums give neighbors leverage, and the recent record shows more disputes about keeping/renewing existing yards than approvals of new ones.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] c.140 §59: "No original license or permit shall be issued hereunder to a person within said class 3 until after a hearing", "of which seven days' notice shall have been given to the owners of the property abutting" the premises — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59).
- [VERIFIED] c.140 §59: "Cities and towns by ordinance or by-law may regulate the situation of the premises of licensees within class 3"; the applicant must have "a place of business suitable for the purpose"; the statute text itself does not mention zoning — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59).
- [VERIFIED] Licenses "shall expire on January first following the date of issue unless sooner revoked"; the board "may, in its discretion, waive the annual hearing for renewal of a class 3 license" (except Boston); licenses shall be revoked after hearing for non-compliance with c.140 §§57-69; after revocation, a new license for the same person or premises requires the Registrar's approval — [MGL c.140 §59](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59).
- [VERIFIED] Ashfield (2025-26): neighbor appeal of building commissioner's Nov. 2025 ruling that a junkyard is grandfathered; hearings March 26 and April 23, 2026 with about two hours of public comment; standing disputed (appellant lives over a mile away); earlier 2022 cleanup order; owner offered fencing — [Greenfield Recorder, Apr. 24 2026](https://recorder.com/2026/04/24/zoning-board-ashfield-junkyard/). In May 2026 the ZBA proposed mediation between the junkyard owners and the two appealing residents — [Greenfield Recorder, May 1 2026](https://recorder.com/2026/05/01/mediation-ashfield-junkyard-appeal/) [SNIPPET].
- [SNIPPET] Springfield (Aug. 2021): City Council voted to approve a Pick-n-Pull self-service auto parts yard despite community concern — [KTVZ/CNN regional](https://ktvz.com/cnn-regional/2021/08/16/community-concerned-after-springfield-city-council-voted-to-approve-pick-n-pull/) (page 403; details unverified).
- [SNIPPET] Framingham selectmen declined to renew a salvage license citing dust and rust from metal piles blowing into the neighborhood (older, undated) — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/salvage-site-on-shaky-ground-).
- [VERIFIED] Freetown 2016: residents petitioned the ZBA over traffic and noise to stop Excel Recycling's shredder; ZBA rejected the petition; opponents including a competitor also appealed the MassDEP air permit — [Waste Dive](https://www.wastedive.com/news/excel-recycling-gets-green-light-for-construction-of-scrap-facility-in-mass/426404/).
- [SNIPPET] RI: Woonsocket council renewal fight and resulting lawsuit; Foster $100/day fines and ZBR denial (see section 2) — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/ri-officials-support-license-for-salvage-yard); [Justia RI summaries](https://zoningplanninglanduseopinions.justia.com/category/rhode-island-supreme-court/).

### Inferences
- A new yard faces at least three public votes/hearings where abutters are notified: zoning special permit (supermajority), Class 3 license hearing, and often conservation commission/site plan. The annual January 1 expiry also gives opponents a recurring forum (Framingham, Woonsocket examples), so compliance history is part of an existing yard's value.
- The cases found are overwhelmingly about enforcement against, or renewal of, existing yards; I found no 2024-2026 example of a brand-new open-air auto salvage yard being approved inside the Route 128/495 belt, which is consistent with (but does not prove) the view that new yards there are rare.
- Competitors, not just residents, use permit appeals (Freetown).

### Gaps
- No reported decision was retrieved directly holding that a Class 3 license cannot issue without zoning compliance; the relationship is inferred from §59's "situation of the premises" clause and local practice. Many licensing boards require a zoning sign-off — unverified for Waltham.
- Outcome of the Ashfield appeal/mediation after May 2026: not found.
- Details and dates of the Framingham and Woonsocket matters not verified (article bodies not retrievable).

## 6. Is indoor dismantling (in a warehouse) treated differently from an open-air yard?

### Takeaway
Yes at the state-licensing level, not necessarily at the zoning level: both MGL c.140 §59A and RIGL §5-21-4 list operation "entirely within a building" as a stand-alone way to satisfy siting conditions (no distance or fencing requirement), but local zoning may still classify indoor dismantling as a junkyard/auto recycling center requiring the same special permit (Waltham expressly equates "automobile recycling center" with "junkyard").

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] MA: a Class 3 yard operated entirely within a building satisfies §59A without meeting the highway/park/school separations or screening requirements — [MGL c.140 §59A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXX/Chapter140/Section59A).
- [VERIFIED] RI: same structure; the first qualifying condition is that the junkyard be operated and maintained entirely within a building — [RIGL §5-21-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE5/5-21/5-21-4.htm).
- [VERIFIED] Waltham: "Automobile recycling center: See Junkyard"; both require City Council special permit; no indoor exemption seen in the text read — [Waltham zoning ch. 3, Zoneomics](https://www.zoneomics.com/code/waltham-MA/chapter_3).
- [VERIFIED] Worcester: the special-permit category is defined as "OPEN LOT storage ... wrecking and/or dismantling of motor vehicles" — i.e., defined by an open-lot location — while manufacturing/processing in buildings and auto body shops are by right in ML/MG districts and repairs must be inside a building — [Worcester Zoning Ordinance PDF](https://www.worcesterma.gov/planning-regulatory/document-center/zoning-ord.pdf).
- [VERIFIED] 310 CMR 22.21(2)(a)3 keys its Zone II prohibition to "automobile graveyards and junkyard, as defined in M.G.L. c. 140B, § 1" — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/310-CMR-22-21).

### Inferences
- In a code drafted like Worcester's, a fully enclosed dismantling operation might be argued to fall outside "open lot storage" and inside an industrial/processing or repair category, but that is an interpretive question for the building commissioner/ZBA, not an established rule; outdoor storage of hulks would pull it back into the special-permit category. In Waltham the text points the other way (recycling center = junkyard).
- Indoor operation also weakens the usual opposition arguments (visual blight, dust, stormwater contact) and simplifies §59A compliance, so it is the most plausible path for a new entrant; the Class 3 license and hearing are still required for anyone buying vehicles to dismantle for parts.

### Gaps
- No MA/RI decision or zoning determination was found squarely addressing whether an all-indoor dismantler is a "junkyard" for zoning purposes; no municipal code with an explicit separate "indoor auto recycling" use category was found.
- Definition in MGL c.140B §1 (and whether it reaches indoor facilities) was not fetched.
